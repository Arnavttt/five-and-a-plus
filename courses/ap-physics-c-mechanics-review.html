How to Get a 5 on This Exam

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

AP Physics C Mechanics is calculus-based and covers kinematics (with calculus), Newton's laws, work/energy theorem, momentum, rotational mechanics (with full tensor treatment), oscillations, and gravitation. FRQs require calculus integration and differentiation. You must be able to derive equations from first principles using Newton's laws and energy methods, set up and solve differential equations for SHM and rotational systems.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Students forget to use calculus where required writing v = at instead of v = ∫a dt when acceleration is non-constant. Students also confuse moment of inertia formulas for different geometries (solid sphere vs. hollow sphere vs. rod). In rotational problems, students forget that I must be calculated about the correct axis using the parallel axis theorem. Students lose points by not showing integration steps.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

A 3 student uses calculus only when forced. A 5 student automatically reaches for integration when acceleration varies with time or position, uses energy methods when forces do work over distances, and uses impulse-momentum for impulsive forces. Choosing the most efficient method and showing clear calculus work distinguishes 5-level responses.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Derive every major equation yourself: v(t), x(t) from variable acceleration; work-energy theorem via integration; conservation of angular momentum for system of particles; SHM differential equation and solution. Review the parallel axis theorem. Practice 3 full FRQs per week under exam conditions (45 min for 3 questions). Know all moment of inertia formulas.

Exam-Day Skills

  • Calculus integration protocol: When writing ∫F dt or ∫F dx, always show the integral setup with limits before evaluating. Write out the antiderivative step explicitly graders award partial credit for correct setup even with arithmetic errors.
  • Rotational dynamics checklist: Identify moment of inertia (with correct axis via parallel axis theorem if needed), set up Στ = Iα, and check whether angular momentum is conserved (zero net external torque).
  • SHM identification: A restoring force proportional to displacement (F = −kx) always produces SHM. Set up the differential equation: d²x/dt² = −ω²x, where ω² = k/m. Solution: x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ).
  • Energy methods: When forces are position-dependent, use work-energy theorem (∫F dx). When problem asks for velocity at a specific position, energy methods are almost always faster than kinematics.
  • FRQ justification: For qualitative questions, cite the specific law or principle (e.g., 'by conservation of angular momentum, since no external torque acts on the system...') before stating the conclusion.

Essential Concepts

  • Kinematics: equations of motion, calculus forms (v = dx/dt, a = dv/dt)
  • Newton's laws: force analysis, constraint problems (pulleys, inclines)
  • Work and energy: work-energy theorem, conservation of mechanical energy
  • Impulse and momentum: collisions (elastic, inelastic), rocket propulsion
  • Rotation: rolling without slipping, parallel axis theorem, precession

Exam Day Tips

✓

FRQ setup: draw a diagram, define a coordinate system, and write your equation of motion before solving.

✓

Simple harmonic motion: know ω² = k/m for spring and ω² = g/L for pendulum.

✓

Rolling without slipping: v_cm = rω and a_cm = rα these links always hold.

✓

Memorize standard moments of inertia: solid sphere (2/5 MR²), hoop (MR²), solid disk (1/2 MR²).

📋 How FRQ Scoring Works

Section I (50%): 35 MCQs in 45 min. Section II (50%): 3 FRQs in 45 min (~15 min each).

FRQ scoring (~15 pts each): Each FRQ is multi-part, covering a complete mechanics scenario. Parts typically include: (a) drawing/labeling a diagram, (b) setting up equations using calculus or Newton's laws, (c) solving for a quantity, (d) qualitative analysis or prediction.

Calculus requirement: Integration and differentiation are required throughout. Setting up an integral (with correct integrand and limits) earns points even if evaluation contains arithmetic errors. Showing the integration steps explicitly is essential: a final answer without work earns minimal or no credit.

Partial credit: Graders award credit for each correctly completed element. A wrong answer in part (b) does not prevent you from earning full credit in part (c) if you correctly use the wrong answer from (b): they apply 'follow-through' credit.

Justification questions: 'Justify your answer using physics principles' requires citing the specific law (conservation of angular momentum, Newton's 3rd law, work-energy theorem) before applying it. Never state a conclusion without the principle that supports it.

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. For a particle moving in a circle of radius r at speed v, the centripetal acceleration magnitude is:

  • A. v/r
  • B. v²/r
  • C. vr
  • D. v²r
Show Answer

Answer: B: Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r, directed toward the center. It arises from the continuous change in direction of the velocity vector, not a change in speed.

Q2. The work-energy theorem states:

  • A. W_net = ΔKE (net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy)
  • B. W = Fd cos θ only for constant forces
  • C. Energy is always conserved
  • D. W = mgh for all situations
Show Answer

Answer: A: Work-energy theorem: the net work done on an object by all forces equals its change in kinetic energy. W_net = KE_f - KE_i = ΔKE.

Q3. For a system of particles, the center of mass acceleration equals:

  • A. The average acceleration of all particles
  • B. The net external force on the system divided by total mass (F_net = Ma_cm)
  • C. Zero if no external forces act
  • D. The acceleration of the heaviest particle
Show Answer

Answer: B: Newton's 2nd law for systems: F_net_external = Ma_cm. Internal forces cancel by Newton's 3rd law; only external forces accelerate the center of mass.

Q4. Conservation of angular momentum applies when:

  • A. A net torque acts on the system
  • B. No net external torque acts on the system
  • C. The moment of inertia is constant
  • D. Only when the system rotates at constant speed
Show Answer

Answer: B: Angular momentum L = Iω is conserved when the net external torque is zero analogous to linear momentum conservation when net force is zero. A spinning figure skater pulling in their arms decreases I, so ω must increase.

Q5. The moment of inertia of a uniform solid sphere about an axis through its center is:

  • A. (1/2)MR²
  • B. (2/5)MR²
  • C. MR²
  • D. (2/3)MR²
Show Answer

Answer: B: Standard results: solid sphere = (2/5)MR²; hollow sphere = (2/3)MR²; solid cylinder = (1/2)MR²; thin rod about center = (1/12)ML². These require calculus to derive via ∫r²dm.

Q6. The parallel axis theorem states that I = I_cm + Md², where d is:

  • A. The radius of the object
  • B. The distance from the center of mass to the new rotation axis
  • C. The distance between two parallel axes
  • D. Half the object's length
Show Answer

Answer: B: Parallel axis theorem: the moment of inertia about any axis parallel to the axis through the CM equals I_cm plus Md² (mass times square of distance between axes).

Q7. For a spring-mass system undergoing SHM, the angular frequency ω equals:

  • A. m/k
  • B. √(k/m)
  • C. 2π/T
  • D. Both B and C are correct
Show Answer

Answer: D: ω = √(k/m) by analyzing the equation of motion from F = -kx = ma. Also, ω = 2π/T (definition of angular frequency relating to period T). Both are correct expressions for ω.

Q8. Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times) is a consequence of:

  • A. Newton's law of gravitation
  • B. Conservation of angular momentum (zero torque from central gravitational force)
  • C. Conservation of energy
  • D. The elliptical shape of orbits
Show Answer

Answer: B: Gravitational force is central (directed toward the Sun, producing zero torque about the Sun). Therefore L = r × p is conserved for orbiting bodies, which geometrically requires equal areas swept in equal times.

Q9. The rotational kinetic energy of an object with moment of inertia I and angular velocity ω is:

  • A. Iω
  • B. (1/2)Iω²
  • C. Iω²
  • D. (1/2)mv²
Show Answer

Answer: B: Rotational KE = (1/2)Iω² directly analogous to translational KE = (1/2)mv², with I replacing m and ω replacing v.

Q10. For a perfectly inelastic collision, which quantity is conserved?

  • A. Kinetic energy only
  • B. Linear momentum only
  • C. Both kinetic energy and linear momentum
  • D. Neither kinetic energy nor linear momentum
Show Answer

Answer: B: In all collisions, linear momentum is conserved (no external forces). In perfectly inelastic collisions, kinetic energy is NOT conserved objects stick together and maximum kinetic energy is lost (converted to deformation, heat, sound).

Q11. The gravitational potential energy between two masses M and m separated by distance r is:

  • A. GMm/r
  • B. −GMm/r
  • C. GMm/r²
  • D. −GMm/r²
Show Answer

Answer: B: Gravitational PE = −GMm/r (negative by convention, with U=0 at r=∞). The negative sign reflects that gravity is attractive you must do work to separate masses to infinity, increasing their PE from negative toward zero.

Q12. Which of the following correctly states the impulse-momentum theorem?

  • A. Impulse equals average force times displacement
  • B. Impulse J = FΔt = Δp (change in momentum)
  • C. Impulse equals the change in kinetic energy
  • D. Impulse is always in the direction of motion
Show Answer

Answer: B: Impulse-momentum theorem: J = ∫F dt = Δp. Impulse (force × time) equals the change in momentum. This is Newton's 2nd law integrated over time.

Q13. For rolling without slipping, the velocity of the contact point with the ground is:

  • A. Equal to the center's velocity
  • B. Zero (the contact point is instantaneously at rest)
  • C. Twice the center's velocity
  • D. In the direction opposite to motion
Show Answer

Answer: B: In rolling without slipping: v_contact = v_cm - ωr = v_cm - v_cm = 0. The contact point is instantaneously at rest relative to the ground. The top of the wheel has speed 2v_cm.

Q14. The escape velocity from a planet of mass M and radius R is:

  • A. √(GM/R)
  • B. √(2GM/R)
  • C. GM/R
  • D. 2GM/R
Show Answer

Answer: B: Setting total mechanical energy to zero at infinity: (1/2)mv² - GMm/R = 0 → v_escape = √(2GM/R). Earth's escape velocity is ~11.2 km/s.

Q15. Simple harmonic motion is defined by which differential equation?

  • A. d²x/dt² = kx
  • B. d²x/dt² = -ω²x
  • C. d²x/dt² = -kx/m²
  • D. dx/dt = -ωx
Show Answer

Answer: B: SHM: the restoring force is proportional to and opposite displacement → F = -kx = ma → a = -(k/m)x → d²x/dt² = -ω²x where ω² = k/m. Solutions: x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ).

Q16. Gauss's law analog in mechanics the shell theorem states that:

  • A. A spherical shell exerts no gravitational force anywhere
  • B. A uniform spherical shell exerts zero net gravitational force on objects INSIDE it, and acts as if all its mass were at its center for objects OUTSIDE
  • C. Gravitational force decreases linearly inside a solid sphere
  • D. All mass can always be treated as a point mass at the center
Show Answer

Answer: B: Shell theorem: a uniform spherical shell attracts external objects as if all mass concentrated at center. Inside a spherical shell: zero net force. Inside a uniform solid sphere: only the mass within radius r contributes.

Q17. The gradient of potential energy gives:

  • A. The kinetic energy at that point
  • B. The negative of the force: F = -∇U
  • C. The acceleration directly
  • D. The work done by external forces
Show Answer

Answer: B: Conservative forces are related to potential energy by F = -dU/dx (1D) or F = -∇U (3D). The force points in the direction of decreasing potential energy, with magnitude equal to the rate of decrease.

Q18. A rigid body is in static equilibrium when:

  • A. Only the net force is zero
  • B. Only the net torque about the center of mass is zero
  • C. Both the net force AND net torque about any point are zero
  • D. Its velocity is constant
Show Answer

Answer: C: Static equilibrium requires: ΣF = 0 (translational equilibrium) AND Στ = 0 about any axis (rotational equilibrium). Both conditions must hold simultaneously.

Q19. The coefficient of restitution e for a collision is defined as:

  • A. The ratio of kinetic energies before and after collision
  • B. The ratio of relative speeds after to relative speeds before collision (e = |v₂'-v₁'|/|v₁-v₂|)
  • C. The fraction of momentum conserved
  • D. The ratio of impulses exchanged
Show Answer

Answer: B: e = relative speed of separation / relative speed of approach. e = 1: perfectly elastic (no KE lost). e = 0: perfectly inelastic (objects stick). 0 < e < 1: partially inelastic (real collisions).

Q20. The period of a physical pendulum (extended rigid body) of moment of inertia I about the pivot, mass m, with CM at distance d from pivot is:

  • A. T = 2π√(L/g) for any shape
  • B. T = 2π√(I/mgd)
  • C. T = 2π√(mgd/I)
  • D. T = 2π√(md²/g)
Show Answer

Answer: B: Physical pendulum period: T = 2π√(I/mgd). For a simple pendulum: I = mL², d = L → T = 2π√(mL²/mgL) = 2π√(L/g). The physical pendulum generalizes this to any rigid body.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (FRQ)

A uniform disk of mass M = 2 kg and radius R = 0.5 m is free to rotate about a fixed horizontal axis through its center. A string is wrapped around the rim and attached to a hanging mass m = 1 kg. (a) Draw a free body diagram for both the disk and the hanging mass. (b) Write Newton's second law equations for both the mass and the disk (using torque). (c) Solve for the angular acceleration of the disk and the linear acceleration of the hanging mass. (d) Find the tension in the string.
View Model Answer
(a) FBDs: Hanging mass m: Weight mg downward, Tension T upward. Disk: Tension T applied tangentially at rim (creates torque), axle reaction force upward (no torque about center), weight Mg downward through center (no torque about center). (b) Newton's 2nd law: For hanging mass (taking downward as positive): mg - T = ma For disk (net torque = Iα, counterclockwise positive): TR = Iα = (1/2)MR², α Constraint (no slipping of string): a = αR (c) Solving: From disk equation: T = (1/2)MRα = (1/2)Mα R. Substitute constraint a = αR → T = (1/2)Ma. Substitute into mass equation: mg - (1/2)Ma = ma → mg = a(m + M/2) → a = mg/(m + M/2) = (1×10)/(1 + 1) = 5 m/s². Angular acceleration: α = a/R = 5/0.5 = 10 rad/s². (d) Tension: T = (1/2)Ma = (1/2)(2)(5) = 5 N. (Check: mg - T = 10 - 5 = 5 = ma = 1×5. ✓)

FRQ 2 (FRQ)

A satellite of mass m orbits a planet of mass M in a circular orbit of radius r. (a) Derive an expression for the orbital speed v in terms of G, M, and r. (b) Show that the period T = 2π√(r³/GM): Kepler's third law. (c) A satellite is moved from orbit r₁ to r₂ > r₁. Determine whether work done on the satellite is positive, negative, or zero, and explain using total mechanical energy.
View Model Answer
(a) Orbital speed: Gravity provides centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r → v² = GM/r → v = √(GM/r). Note: faster planet → smaller orbit (v increases as r decreases). (b) Kepler's third law: Period T = circumference / speed = 2πr/v = 2πr/√(GM/r) = 2πr, √(r/GM) = 2π√(r³/GM). Therefore T² = 4π²r³/GM → T² ∝ r³. This is Kepler's third law derived from Newton's law of gravitation. (c) Total mechanical energy: E = KE + PE = (1/2)mv² + (-GMm/r) = (1/2)m(GM/r) - GMm/r = -GMm/(2r). Since E = -GMm/(2r) and r₂ > r₁: |E₂| < |E₁| and both are negative, so E₂ > E₁ (less negative). Moving to a higher orbit requires POSITIVE work done on the satellite (total energy increases, becomes less negative). This is counterintuitive: the satellite slows down (less KE) but gains more PE than it loses in KE, so net energy input is required.

FRQ 3 (FRQ)

A block of mass m = 3 kg is attached to a spring (k = 200 N/m) on a frictionless horizontal surface. The block is displaced 0.1 m from equilibrium and released. (a) Find the angular frequency, period, and maximum speed. (b) Write the position as a function of time x(t), choosing t = 0 at maximum displacement. (c) At what position(s) does the block have half its maximum kinetic energy?
View Model Answer
(a) Angular frequency: ω = √(k/m) = √(200/3) = √66.7 = 8.16 rad/s. Period: T = 2π/ω = 2π/8.16 = 0.770 s. Maximum speed (at equilibrium): KE_max = PE_max → (1/2)mv²_max = (1/2)kA² → v_max = A√(k/m) = 0.1×8.16 = 0.816 m/s. (b) Starting at maximum displacement (x = A = 0.1 m at t = 0): x(t) = A cos(ωt) = 0.1 cos(8.16t) meters. (c) At half maximum KE: (1/2)KE_max = (1/4)kA². Using energy conservation: (1/2)kx² + KE = (1/2)kA². KE = (1/2)kA² - (1/2)kx². Set KE = (1/4)kA²: (1/4)kA² = (1/2)kA² - (1/2)kx² → (1/2)kx² = (1/4)kA² → x² = A²/2 → x = ±A/√2 = ±0.1/√2 = ±0.0707 m. The block has half its maximum kinetic energy at x = ±7.07 cm from equilibrium.

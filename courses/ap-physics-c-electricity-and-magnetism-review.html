How to Get a 5 on This Exam

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

AP Physics C E&M is calculus-based and covers electrostatics (Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, electric potential), conductors, capacitors, DC circuits, magnetic fields (Biot-Savart, Ampere's law), electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law, Lenz's law), inductors, and Maxwell's equations. FRQs require setting up and evaluating integrals, applying vector calculus concepts, and deriving expressions.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Students fail to specify Gaussian surfaces correctly a Gaussian surface must have the field either parallel or perpendicular to the surface everywhere, and the field must be uniform over the surface. Students misapply Ampere's law by not identifying the correct Amperian loop. For Faraday's law, students forget to account for the sign (Lenz's law) and write magnitudes only.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

A 3 student applies Gauss's law and Faraday's law to memorized configurations. A 5 student can apply them to novel geometries by reasoning about symmetry, setting up the correct integral, and extracting the field expression. Understanding WHY Gauss's law works (and its symmetry requirements) vs. memorizing formula applications is the critical distinction.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Practice Gauss's law for all geometries (sphere, cylinder, infinite plane). Practice Ampere's law for solenoid, toroid, and infinite wire. Derive capacitance expressions for parallel plate, cylindrical, and spherical capacitors from E field. Review RL and RC circuit differential equations and time constants. Do 3 FRQ sets with focus on showing calculus steps.

Exam-Day Skills

  • Gauss's law protocol: (1) Identify symmetry. (2) Choose Gaussian surface matching that symmetry. (3) Argue why E is uniform and perpendicular to surface. (4) Write ∮E, dA = E, A = Q_enc/ε₀. (5) Solve for E.
  • Faraday's law sign: ε = −dΦ_B/dt. The negative sign (Lenz's law) means the induced EMF opposes the change in flux. Use right-hand rule to determine direction of induced current always state your reasoning.
  • Biot-Savart vs. Ampere's: Use Biot-Savart for short wire segments or when there is no Amperian symmetry. Use Ampere's law when high symmetry exists (infinite wire, solenoid, toroid).
  • RC/RL circuits: Know the differential equations and solutions: q(t) = Q(1−e^{−t/RC}) charging; I(t) = I₀e^{−t/τ} discharging/RL. Time constant τ = RC or τ = L/R. At t = 0 capacitors are wires; at t = ∞ capacitors are open circuits.
  • Vector fields: Practice drawing E and B field lines for standard charge distributions. Know E is perpendicular to equipotential surfaces. Graders look for correct field line direction and density.

📋 How FRQ Scoring Works

Section I (50%): 35 MCQs in 45 min. Section II (50%): 3 FRQs in 45 min (~15 min each).

FRQ scoring: Each FRQ covers one major E&M topic (electrostatics, circuits, or magnetism/induction). Parts include diagram labeling, Gauss's law or Ampere's law applications, integral setup and evaluation, circuit analysis (KVL/KCL), and qualitative reasoning about field directions or current flow.

Gauss/Ampere's law FRQs: Earn credit for: (1) identifying the correct surface/loop geometry, (2) arguing why E or B is uniform and perpendicular (or parallel) to the surface, (3) writing the law correctly (∮E, dA = Q_enc/ε₀), (4) correctly evaluating the integral, (5) solving for the field. Each step = points.

Faraday's law FRQs: Earn credit for: identifying flux change, writing ε = −dΦ/dt with correct sign, determining induced current direction via Lenz's law, calculating EMF magnitude, and identifying any induced force or torque.

Follow-through credit: Applies to numerical errors. If your incorrect field expression from part (a) is consistently used in part (b), you earn part (b) credit if the method is correct.

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. Gauss's Law states that the electric flux through a closed surface equals:

  • A. The total charge enclosed divided by ε₀
  • B. Zero for all closed surfaces
  • C. The surface area times the electric field
  • D. The total charge on the surface
Show Answer

Answer: A: Gauss's Law: Φ_E = ∮E, dA = Q_enc/ε₀. The flux through any closed (Gaussian) surface depends only on the total charge enclosed, regardless of surface shape or charge distribution outside.

Q2. For a conducting sphere with charge Q and radius R, the electric field INSIDE the conductor is:

  • A. Q/(4πε₀R²)
  • B. Zero
  • C. kQ/R²
  • D. Uniform and directed outward
Show Answer

Answer: B: In electrostatic equilibrium, the electric field inside a conductor is zero. All excess charge resides on the surface. (Note: for a non-conducting sphere with uniform charge, E inside ≠ 0.)

Q3. The capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor with plate area A and separation d is:

  • A. ε₀A/d
  • B. ε₀d/A
  • C. kA/d
  • D. ε₀A/d²
Show Answer

Answer: A: C = ε₀A/d (for vacuum between plates). Capacitance increases with larger plate area, decreases with larger separation. With dielectric: C = κε₀A/d.

Q4. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) states:

  • A. Current is conserved at each junction
  • B. The sum of all potential differences around any closed loop in a circuit is zero
  • C. Resistance increases with current
  • D. Capacitors block direct current
Show Answer

Answer: B: KVL: ΣΔV = 0 around any closed loop a consequence of energy conservation (electric potential is a state function; the work done around a closed path is zero).

Q5. The magnetic field produced by a long straight wire carrying current I at distance r is:

  • A. μ₀I/r
  • B. μ₀I/(2πr)
  • C. μ₀I/(4πr²)
  • D. μ₀Ir/2π
Show Answer

Answer: B: Ampere's Law for a long straight wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr). The field circles the wire, its magnitude depending inversely on distance from the wire.

Q6. Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction states:

  • A. A moving charge creates a magnetic field
  • B. The induced EMF in a loop equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop (ε = -dΦ_B/dt)
  • C. Magnetic flux through a closed surface is always zero
  • D. Current-carrying wires experience forces in magnetic fields
Show Answer

Answer: B: Faraday's Law: ε = -dΦ_B/dt. The negative sign (Lenz's Law) indicates the induced EMF opposes the change in flux. Changing flux due to changing B, changing area, or changing orientation induces an EMF.

Q7. The energy stored in a capacitor with charge Q and capacitance C is:

  • A. Q/C
  • B. Q²/(2C)
  • C. QC
  • D. Q²C
Show Answer

Answer: B: Energy stored: U = Q²/(2C) = (1/2)CV² = QV/2. All three forms are equivalent via Q = CV. This energy is stored in the electric field between the plates.

Q8. Lenz's Law states that the induced current in a loop:

  • A. Flows in the direction of the applied magnetic field
  • B. Flows in the direction that opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it
  • C. Is proportional to the resistance of the loop
  • D. Has the same direction as the primary current
Show Answer

Answer: B: Lenz's Law (the negative sign in Faraday's Law): the induced current creates a magnetic field that opposes the change in flux. If flux is increasing into the page, induced current flows counterclockwise (field out of page to oppose).

Q9. The self-inductance L of an inductor is defined by:

  • A. L = μ₀N²A/ℓ for a solenoid
  • B. ε = -L(dI/dt)
  • C. Both A and B
  • D. Neither A nor B
Show Answer

Answer: C: Self-inductance L relates induced EMF to rate of current change: ε = -L(dI/dt). For a solenoid: L = μ₀N²A/ℓ (where N = number of turns, A = cross-sectional area, ℓ = length).

Q10. In an RC circuit, the time constant τ represents:

  • A. The time for the capacitor to fully charge
  • B. The time constant = RC, after which the capacitor has charged to (1 - 1/e) ≈ 63% of its final charge
  • C. The period of oscillation
  • D. The resistance times the charge stored
Show Answer

Answer: B: RC time constant τ = RC. Charging: Q(t) = Q_max(1 - e^{-t/RC}). After one time constant t = τ = RC, the charge is (1 - 1/e) ≈ 63% of maximum. After 5τ, essentially fully charged.

Q11. The displacement current, introduced by Maxwell, is:

  • A. The current due to moving free charges in a conductor
  • B. A fictitious current term μ₀ε₀(dΦ_E/dt) that ensures Ampere's Law is consistent in regions with changing electric flux (like between capacitor plates)
  • C. The current in a capacitor's wires
  • D. The average of AC current over a full cycle
Show Answer

Answer: B: Maxwell added the displacement current term to Ampere's Law: ∮B, dl = μ₀(I_enc + ε₀dΦ_E/dt). This term accounts for changing electric fields (between capacitor plates) producing magnetic fields, enabling electromagnetic waves.

Q12. For a magnetic dipole with moment μ in external field B, the potential energy is:

  • A. μB
  • B. −μ, B (minimum when μ parallel to B)
  • C. μ × B
  • D. μ²B
Show Answer

Answer: B: U = -μ, B = -μB cosθ. Minimum energy (most stable) when μ parallel to B (θ = 0, U = -μB). Maximum energy (unstable equilibrium) when μ anti-parallel to B (θ = π, U = +μB).

Q13. Capacitors in series combine as:

  • A. C_total = C₁ + C₂ + ...
  • B. 1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...
  • C. C_total = C₁ × C₂/(C₁ + C₂)
  • D. C_total = 1/(C₁ + C₂)
Show Answer

Answer: B: Capacitors in series: 1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ... (equivalent is smaller than either). Capacitors in parallel: C_total = C₁ + C₂ + ... (equivalent is larger). Opposite of resistors!

Q14. The Hall effect demonstrates:

  • A. That electrons have charge
  • B. The sign and density of charge carriers in a conductor by measuring the transverse voltage when current flows in a magnetic field
  • C. That magnetic fields exert forces on moving charges only
  • D. Electromagnetic induction
Show Answer

Answer: B: Hall effect: when current flows in a conductor in a perpendicular magnetic field, charge carriers experience a transverse force, accumulating on one side and creating a measurable Hall voltage whose sign reveals carrier type (electron or hole).

Q15. The Poynting vector S = (1/μ₀)E × B represents:

  • A. The direction of polarization of an EM wave
  • B. The energy flux density of an electromagnetic field (power per unit area flowing in the direction of propagation)
  • C. The force on a charge in crossed E and B fields
  • D. The amplitude of an EM wave
Show Answer

Answer: B: The Poynting vector S = (1/μ₀)(E × B) gives the instantaneous power per unit area carried by an electromagnetic field, pointing in the direction of energy propagation.

Q16. For the Bohr model of hydrogen, the electron's orbital radius is quantized as:

  • A. r_n = n × a₀
  • B. r_n = n² × a₀ (where a₀ = 0.529 Å is the Bohr radius)
  • C. r_n = a₀/n²
  • D. r_n = n² × R_∞
Show Answer

Answer: B: Bohr model: r_n = n²a₀ where a₀ = 0.529 Å and n = 1, 2, 3, ... The radius grows as n². Energy levels: E_n = -13.6 eV/n².

Q17. In a solenoid with n turns per unit length and current I, the interior magnetic field is:

  • A. μ₀nI/2
  • B. μ₀nI
  • C. μ₀n²I
  • D. 2μ₀nI
Show Answer

Answer: B: Solenoid interior field: B = μ₀nI (where n = N/L = turns per unit length). Derived from Ampere's Law; the field is uniform and axial inside an ideal solenoid, zero outside.

Q18. The energy stored per unit volume in a magnetic field is:

  • A. B²/(2μ₀)
  • B. μ₀B²/2
  • C. B/μ₀
  • D. B²μ₀
Show Answer

Answer: A: Magnetic energy density u_B = B²/(2μ₀). Analogously, electric energy density u_E = ε₀E²/2. For an electromagnetic wave, u_E = u_B (equal energy in electric and magnetic field components).

Q19. When a dielectric is inserted between the plates of a charged, isolated capacitor (disconnected from battery):

  • A. The voltage increases
  • B. The capacitance increases by factor κ, charge stays constant, so voltage decreases by factor κ
  • C. The charge increases
  • D. Nothing changes
Show Answer

Answer: B: Isolated capacitor (Q constant): inserting dielectric κ increases C to κC. Since Q = CV and Q is constant, V decreases to V/κ. Energy U = Q²/(2C) decreases the dielectric is pulled in, converting electrical energy to kinetic energy.

Q20. Electromagnetic waves require:

  • A. A medium (like sound waves)
  • B. No medium they propagate through vacuum because changing E fields produce B fields and vice versa (Maxwell's equations)
  • C. A conductor to propagate along
  • D. A charged particle at the source
Show Answer

Answer: B: Maxwell's equations predict self-sustaining EM waves: a changing E field creates B field (Faraday/displacement current) and changing B field creates E field (Faraday's Law). These mutual dependencies allow propagation without a medium at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (FRQ)

A parallel-plate capacitor has plate area A = 0.02 m² and plate separation d = 0.002 m. A dielectric with κ = 3 is inserted between the plates. (a) Calculate the capacitance with and without the dielectric. (b) The capacitor is connected to a 12V battery and fully charged with the dielectric in place. Calculate the charge stored and energy stored. (c) The battery is disconnected. The dielectric is then removed. Find the new voltage and energy. Explain the energy difference.
View Model Answer
(a) Without dielectric: C₀ = ε₀A/d = (8.85×10⁻¹²)(0.02)/(0.002) = 88.5 pF. With dielectric κ=3: C = κC₀ = 3 × 88.5 = 265.5 pF. (b) With battery at V = 12V and dielectric in place: Charge: Q = CV = (265.5×10⁻¹²)(12) = 3.19×10⁻⁹ C = 3.19 nC. Energy: U = (1/2)CV² = (1/2)(265.5×10⁻¹²)(144) = 1.91×10⁻⁸ J = 19.1 nJ. (c) Battery disconnected (Q is now fixed = 3.19 nC). Dielectric removed: C returns to C₀ = 88.5 pF. New voltage: V' = Q/C₀ = (3.19×10⁻⁹)/(88.5×10⁻¹²) = 36 V = 3 × original voltage. New energy: U' = Q²/(2C₀) = (3.19×10⁻⁹)²/(2×88.5×10⁻¹²) = 10.17×10⁻¹⁸/177×10⁻¹² = 5.74×10⁻⁸ J = 57.4 nJ. Energy increased by 57.4 - 19.1 = 38.3 nJ. The extra energy came from the work done by whoever pulled the dielectric out the electric field attracted the dielectric, so external work was required to remove it, increasing stored electrical energy.

FRQ 2 (FRQ)

A long solenoid has 1000 turns, length 0.5 m, and radius 0.02 m. Current increases uniformly from 0 to 5 A in 0.1 s. (a) Calculate the magnetic field inside the solenoid when I = 5 A. (b) Calculate the self-inductance L of the solenoid. (c) Calculate the induced EMF during the current increase. (d) A second coil of 50 turns is wound around the middle of the solenoid. What is the mutual inductance M, and what EMF is induced in the second coil?
View Model Answer
(a) Interior magnetic field: n = N/L = 1000/0.5 = 2000 turns/m. B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2000)(5) = 4π×10⁻³ × 10⁻⁷⁺³ wait B = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2000)(5) = 4π×10⁻⁷×10⁴ = 4π×10⁻³ ≈ 0.01257 T = 12.57 mT. (b) Self-inductance: L = μ₀N²A/ℓ = (4π×10⁻⁷)(1000²)(π×0.02²)/0.5. A = π(0.02)² = 1.257×10⁻³ m². L = (4π×10⁻⁷)(10⁶)(1.257×10⁻³)/0.5 = (4π×10⁻¹)(1.257×10⁻³)/0.5 = (1.584×10⁻³)/0.5 = 3.16×10⁻³ H ≈ 3.16 mH. (c) Induced EMF: ε = -L(dI/dt) = -(3.16×10⁻³)(5/0.1) = -(3.16×10⁻³)(50) = -0.158 V. Magnitude: 0.158 V. The negative sign indicates the induced EMF opposes the increasing current (Lenz's Law). (d) Mutual inductance: M = μ₀N₁N₂A/ℓ = (4π×10⁻⁷)(1000)(50)(1.257×10⁻³)/0.5 = 3.16×10⁻³ × (50/1000) = 1.58×10⁻⁴ H = 0.158 mH. Induced EMF in second coil: ε₂ = -M(dI/dt) = -(1.58×10⁻⁴)(50) = -7.9×10⁻³ V = -7.9 mV.

FRQ 3 (FRQ)

Use Gauss's Law to find the electric field everywhere for a solid non-conducting sphere of radius R with uniform charge density ρ. (a) Find E for r > R (outside the sphere). (b) Find E for r < R (inside the sphere). (c) Sketch E vs. r and explain the physical meaning of the result at r = R.
View Model Answer
(a) Outside (r > R): Choose a spherical Gaussian surface of radius r > R centered on the sphere. By symmetry, E is radial and constant on this surface. Total charge enclosed: Q_enc = ρ × (4/3)πR³. Gauss's Law: ∮E, dA = Q_enc/ε₀ → E(4πr²) = ρ(4/3)πR³/ε₀ → E = ρR³/(3ε₀r²) = Q/(4πε₀r²). Outside, the sphere behaves exactly like a point charge Q at the center. (b) Inside (r < R): Gaussian surface of radius r < R. Charge enclosed: Q_enc = ρ × (4/3)πr³ (only the charge within radius r contributes). Gauss's Law: E(4πr²) = ρ(4/3)πr³/ε₀ → E = ρr/(3ε₀). Inside, E increases linearly with r from zero at the center. (c) Sketch: E = 0 at r = 0, increases linearly to E_max = ρR/(3ε₀) at r = R (from inside). For r > R, E decreases as 1/r² from the same value E_max = ρR³/(3ε₀R²) = ρR/(3ε₀). The field is continuous at r = R (no surface charge on this non-conductor), with a smooth maximum at the surface. Physical meaning: at the surface, maximum field because inside the charge from the sphere contributes linearly (grows with r³ faster than surface area grows with r²), while outside the 1/r² drop dominates. The surface is where the balance shifts from 'more enclosed charge' dominating to 'spreading over larger area' dominating.

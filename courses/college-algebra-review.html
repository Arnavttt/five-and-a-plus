How to Score 100 on College Algebra

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

Solving equations and inequalities, working with functions, and applying algebraic concepts to real problems. Your instructor will heavily test algebraic manipulation, function notation, and word problems.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Arithmetic errors in multi-step problems, forgetting to check solutions (especially in rational and radical equations), and not showing work clearly. Partial credit depends on legible, organized work.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

Students who score 100 show every algebraic step clearly, double-check answers by substituting back, and understand WHY each step works: not just how to perform it mechanically.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Re-do every homework problem from scratch without notes. Focus on problem types you missed on quizzes. Practice solving each equation type (linear, quadratic, rational, radical) until it feels automatic.

Exam-Day Skills

  • Always check your solution by plugging back into the original equation
  • Show every step: instructors give partial credit for correct process
  • For functions: practice domain, range, composition, and inverse until automatic
  • For sequences: know the explicit formula vs. the recursive formula
  • Label your work: write what operation you're doing at each step
  • For word problems: define your variable first, then set up the equation

Exam Day Tips

✓

Memorize the quadratic formula and when to use it vs. factoring.

✓

For log problems, use change-of-base: log_b(x) = ln(x)/ln(b).

✓

Conic sections: complete the square to convert to standard form.

✓

Systems of equations: use substitution for 2×2, matrices (row reduction) for 3×3.

Final Reminder: Review each unit's must-know section, then come back here to check your vocabulary and concepts. Good luck: you've got this! 🎯

📋 How Free Response Scoring Works

Section I: Multiple choice covering all major function types, equations, inequalities, and systems. Section II: Free-response requiring algebraic work, graph interpretation, and application problems.

Scoring principles:
• Show all algebraic steps. Numerical answers without supporting work earn 0 to 1 pts. Each algebraic manipulation is an opportunity to earn partial credit.
• Check answers in the original equation. Extraneous solutions in radical or logarithmic equations lose full credit for that part. Always verify solutions by substitution.
• Function analysis questions: When asked to find domain, range, or asymptotes, state your reasoning explicitly: 'Domain: denominator ≠ 0, so x ≠ 2, x ≠ −3. Domain: all real numbers except 2 and −3.'
• Graphing questions: Plot key features first (intercepts, vertex, asymptotes), then sketch the curve. Label all key points with coordinates.
• Systems of equations: Show elimination or substitution steps clearly. State the solution as an ordered pair (x, y) and verify in BOTH original equations.

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. The solution set of |2x - 5| < 3 is:

  • A. x < 1 or x > 4
  • B. 1 < x < 4
  • C. x < 4
  • D. x > 1
Show Answer

Answer: B: |2x-5| < 3 means -3 < 2x-5 < 3. Add 5: 2 < 2x < 8. Divide by 2: 1 < x < 4.

Q2. The domain of f(x) = √(x-4)/(x-7) is:

  • A. x ≥ 4
  • B. x ≥ 4 and x ≠ 7
  • C. x > 4
  • D. All real numbers except x = 7
Show Answer

Answer: B: For the square root: x - 4 ≥ 0 → x ≥ 4. For the denominator: x - 7 ≠ 0 → x ≠ 7. Intersection: x ≥ 4 and x ≠ 7, written [4, 7) ∪ (7, ∞).

Q3. Completing the square on x² + 6x + 2 gives:

  • A. (x+3)² + 2
  • B. (x+3)² - 7
  • C. (x+6)² + 2
  • D. (x+3)² - 11
Show Answer

Answer: B: x² + 6x + 2: take half of 6 (= 3), square it (9), add and subtract: x² + 6x + 9 - 9 + 2 = (x+3)² - 7.

Q4. A system of two linear equations has NO solution when:

  • A. The lines are perpendicular
  • B. The lines are parallel (same slope, different y-intercepts)
  • C. The lines intersect at the origin
  • D. The lines are identical
Show Answer

Answer: B: Parallel lines (same slope, different intercepts) never intersect → no solution (inconsistent system). Identical lines → infinitely many solutions. Intersecting lines → exactly one solution.

Q5. The inverse of the matrix A = [[1,2],[3,4]] is:

  • A. [[4,-2],[-3,1]]
  • B. [[-2,1],[1.5,-0.5]]
  • C. [[4,2],[3,1]]
  • D. The matrix has no inverse
Show Answer

Answer: B: det(A) = 1×4 - 2×3 = 4-6 = -2. A⁻¹ = (1/det)×[[d,-b],[-c,a]] = (1/-2)×[[4,-2],[-3,1]] = [[-2,1],[3/2,-1/2]].

Q6. The graph of y = f(x-3) + 2 compared to y = f(x) is:

  • A. Shifted left 3 and up 2
  • B. Shifted right 3 and up 2
  • C. Shifted right 3 and down 2
  • D. Reflected and shifted
Show Answer

Answer: B: Transformations: y = f(x - h) + k shifts the graph h units right (h > 0) and k units up (k > 0). So f(x-3) + 2 shifts right 3 and up 2.

Q7. Synthetic division divides a polynomial by:

  • A. Any polynomial
  • B. A linear factor of the form (x - c)
  • C. A quadratic factor
  • D. A constant only
Show Answer

Answer: B: Synthetic division is a shortcut for dividing a polynomial by a linear divisor (x - c). It uses only coefficients, making it faster than long division for this case.

Q8. The rational root theorem states that if p/q (in lowest terms) is a rational root of aₙxⁿ + ... + a₀, then:

  • A. p divides aₙ and q divides a₀
  • B. p divides a₀ and q divides aₙ
  • C. p and q divide the leading coefficient
  • D. p/q must equal 1 or -1
Show Answer

Answer: B: Rational Root Theorem: possible rational roots are p/q where p divides the constant term a₀ and q divides the leading coefficient aₙ. This gives a finite list of candidates to test.

Q9. An exponential function f(x) = 2ˣ differs from a power function g(x) = x² in that:

  • A. Exponential functions are always larger
  • B. In exponential functions, the variable is in the exponent; in power functions, the variable is the base
  • C. Exponential functions cannot be negative
  • D. Power functions grow faster for all x
Show Answer

Answer: B: Exponential f(x) = bˣ: constant base, variable exponent. Grows faster than any power function for large x. Power function g(x) = xⁿ: variable base, constant exponent. For large x, exponential always dominates.

Q10. If log₂(x) = 5, then x =

  • A. 10
  • B. 32
  • C. 25
  • D. 2/5
Show Answer

Answer: B: log₂(x) = 5 means 2⁵ = x → x = 32.

Q11. Which of the following represents an arithmetic sequence?

  • A. 2, 4, 8, 16, ...
  • B. 5, 8, 11, 14, ...
  • C. 1, 2, 4, 7, ...
  • D. 3, 9, 27, ...
Show Answer

Answer: B: Arithmetic sequence: constant difference between consecutive terms. 8-5=3, 11-8=3, 14-11=3. Geometric sequences have a constant ratio (2, 4, 8, 16).

Q12. Cramer's Rule solves a system Ax = b using:

  • A. Row reduction only
  • B. Determinants: x_i = det(A_i)/det(A) where A_i has column i replaced by b
  • C. Matrix inversion with the identity matrix
  • D. Gaussian elimination with back-substitution
Show Answer

Answer: B: Cramer's Rule: for Ax = b, x_i = det(A_i)/det(A) where A_i is the matrix A with the ith column replaced by vector b. Valid when det(A) ≠ 0.

Q13. The vertex of the parabola y = -2(x+1)² + 5 is:

  • A. (-1, 5) and the parabola opens downward
  • B. (1, 5) and opens upward
  • C. (-1, -5) and opens upward
  • D. (1, -5) and opens downward
Show Answer

Answer: A: Vertex form y = a(x-h)² + k: vertex at (h, k) = (-1, 5) (note x+1 = x-(-1) so h = -1). a = -2 < 0 so the parabola opens downward.

Q14. The Remainder Theorem states that when polynomial P(x) is divided by (x-c):

  • A. The remainder is always 0
  • B. The remainder equals P(c)
  • C. The quotient has degree one less than P
  • D. c is always a root of P(x)
Show Answer

Answer: B: Remainder Theorem: if P(x) is divided by (x-c), the remainder is P(c). Corollary: if P(c) = 0, then (x-c) is a factor of P(x) (Factor Theorem).

Q15. To solve 3^(2x+1) = 27, you would:

  • A. Take the log of both sides
  • B. Recognize 27 = 3³ and set 2x+1 = 3, giving x = 1
  • C. Use the natural logarithm
  • D. Divide both sides by 3
Show Answer

Answer: B: 27 = 3³. So 3^(2x+1) = 3³ → 2x+1 = 3 → 2x = 2 → x = 1. When bases are equal, exponents must be equal.

Q16. A function's behavior as x → ±∞ is called:

  • A. Its range
  • B. Its end behavior
  • C. Its asymptotic behavior
  • D. Its limit at a point
Show Answer

Answer: B: End behavior: how f(x) behaves as x approaches positive or negative infinity. Determined by the degree and leading coefficient for polynomials; by the horizontal asymptote for rational functions.

Q17. Which of the following is a factor of x³ - 8?

  • A. x - 2
  • B. x + 2
  • C. x - 4
  • D. x² - 2x + 4
Show Answer

Answer: A: x³ - 8 = x³ - 2³ is a difference of cubes: (x-2)(x² + 2x + 4). So (x-2) is a factor. The quadratic factor x² + 2x + 4 has no real roots (discriminant = 4 - 16 < 0).

Q18. The sum of an infinite geometric series S = a/(1-r) requires:

  • A. a > 1
  • B. │r│ < 1 (the common ratio's absolute value must be less than 1)
  • C. r > 0
  • D. a = 1
Show Answer

Answer: B: Infinite geometric series converges (has a finite sum) only when |r| < 1. S = a/(1-r). If |r| ≥ 1, the series diverges (no finite sum).

Q19. Descartes' Rule of Signs for f(x) = x³ - 4x² + x + 6 predicts:

  • A. Exactly 3 positive real roots
  • B. Either 2 or 0 positive real roots (2 sign changes)
  • C. Exactly 1 positive real root
  • D. No real roots
Show Answer

Answer: B: Count sign changes in f(x) = x³ - 4x² + x + 6: (+)(-)→1, (-)( +)→2, (+)(+)→0. Two sign changes → 2 or 0 positive real roots. For negative roots, evaluate f(-x) = -x³ - 4x² - x + 6: sign changes once → 1 negative real root.

Q20. The solution to the inequality x² - x - 6 > 0 is:

  • A. (-∞, -2) ∪ (3, ∞)
  • B. (-2, 3)
  • C. x > 3
  • D. x < -2
Show Answer

Answer: A: x² - x - 6 = (x-3)(x+2) > 0. Sign analysis: positive when both factors positive (x > 3) OR both negative (x < -2). Solution: x < -2 or x > 3, written (-∞, -2) ∪ (3, ∞).

Free Response Questions

Free Response 1 (Free Response)

Solve the system of equations and interpret the result geometrically: 2x + y = 7 4x + 2y = 10
View Model Answer
Solve: Method 1 Elimination. Multiply equation 1 by 2: 4x + 2y = 14. Compare with equation 2: 4x + 2y = 10. Subtracting: 0 = 4. This is a contradiction there is no solution. Geometric interpretation: Both equations represent lines. Equation 1: y = -2x + 7 (slope = -2, y-intercept = 7). Equation 2: 2y = -4x + 10 → y = -2x + 5 (slope = -2, y-intercept = 5). Both lines have slope -2 (parallel) but different y-intercepts (7 vs. 5). Parallel lines never intersect, confirming there is no solution. The system is inconsistent. Comparison: If equation 2 had been 4x + 2y = 14 (double equation 1), the lines would be identical (infinitely many solutions). The critical difference: equations 2 has a different constant term than double equation 1 (10 ≠ 14), making the lines parallel but distinct.

Free Response 2 (Free Response)

Given f(x) = x³ - 3x² - 4x + 12: (a) Use the Rational Root Theorem to list all possible rational roots. (b) Verify that x = 3 is a root using synthetic division. (c) Factor f(x) completely over the reals and find all zeros.
View Model Answer
(a) Rational Root Theorem: possible rational roots = ±(factors of 12)/(factors of 1) = ±1, ±2, ±3, ±4, ±6, ±12. (b) Synthetic division by (x-3): 3 | 1 -3 -4 12 | 3 0 -12 ───────────── 1 0 -4 0 Remainder = 0 → x = 3 is a root. ✓ Quotient: x² + 0x - 4 = x² - 4. (c) So f(x) = (x-3)(x² - 4) = (x-3)(x-2)(x+2). Zeros: x = 3, x = 2, x = -2. All three are real zeros of multiplicity 1, so the graph crosses the x-axis at each. Check: f(0) = 0 - 0 - 0 + 12 = 12 (y-intercept). End behavior: leading term x³ → as x→+∞, f→+∞; as x→-∞, f→-∞.

Free Response 3 (Free Response)

a) Explain the difference between exponential growth and exponential decay, giving a real-world example of each. b) A radioactive substance has a half-life of 10 years. Write an exponential decay function for the amount A(t) remaining from an initial amount A₀. c) How long will it take for 90% of the substance to decay (only 10% remains)? Show your work.
View Model Answer
a) Exponential growth: A(t) = A₀ | bᵗ where b > 1. The quantity grows at a rate proportional to its current size the more there is, the faster it grows. Example: compound interest (A(t) = P(1+r)ᵗ) or bacterial population doubling every 20 minutes. Exponential decay: A(t) = A₀ | bᵗ where 0 < b < 1. The quantity decreases at a rate proportional to its current size the less there is, the slower it decreases. Example: radioactive decay, depreciation of a car's value, drug concentration in the bloodstream. b) Half-life 10 years: after 10 years, half remains. A(10) = A₀/2. Using A(t) = A₀ | b^(t/10): A₀ | b^(10/10) = A₀/2 → b = 1/2. Decay function: A(t) = A₀ | (1/2)^(t/10) = A₀ | 2^(-t/10). Equivalently using base e: A(t) = A₀ | e^(-t | ln2/10). c) Find t when A(t) = 0.10 | A₀ (10% remains): 0.10 | A₀ = A₀ | (1/2)^(t/10). Divide by A₀: 0.10 = (1/2)^(t/10). Take log of both sides: log(0.10) = (t/10) | log(0.5). t/10 = log(0.10)/log(0.5) = (-1)/(-0.301) = 3.322. t = 33.2 years. It takes approximately 33.2 years (about 3.32 half-lives) for 90% to decay.

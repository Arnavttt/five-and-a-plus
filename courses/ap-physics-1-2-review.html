How to Get a 5 on This Exam

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

AP Physics 1 tests mechanics (kinematics, Newton's laws, circular motion, energy, momentum, rotation, oscillations, waves, sound) and electric charge/circuits. Physics 2 covers fluids, thermodynamics, electric force/field/potential, capacitors, magnetic fields, electromagnetic induction, optics, and modern physics. Both exams require deriving equations, justifying claims with physics principles, and analyzing experimental data. About 35% of points come from qualitative/conceptual reasoning.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Students apply kinematic equations without checking whether acceleration is constant. They also fail to correctly identify the system in Newton's law problems a free-body diagram must show all forces on ONE object only. In energy problems, students forget to include all energy types (kinetic, gravitational PE, spring PE, thermal). For circuits, students confuse series vs. parallel rules for voltage and current.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

A 3 student can plug numbers into given formulas. A 5 student can derive relationships from first principles, predict the qualitative behavior of a system before calculating, and explain why the physics works not just what the answer is. In FRQs, justification quality matters as much as the correct answer.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Master free-body diagrams draw them for every mechanics problem. Review rotational analogs (torque = rF sinθ, L = Iω, rotational KE = ½Iω²). For Physics 2, review Gauss's Law applications, Lenz's Law, and geometric optics ray diagrams. Do 2 FRQ practice sets per week. Know the difference between AP Physics 1 FRQ types: experimental design, paragraph argument, and derivation.

Exam-Day Skills

  • Free-body diagrams: Label every force with its type and the object exerting it (e.g., F_gravity by Earth on block, F_normal by surface on block). Newton's 3rd law pairs are on DIFFERENT objects never include them on the same FBD.
  • Experimental design FRQ: Identify what to measure, what to vary, what to hold constant, and how to analyze results (often slope of a graph). Explicitly state units for all measured quantities.
  • Paragraph argument: AP Physics 1 has a paragraph-length argument question. Write 3 to 5 sentences explaining the physics principle, applying it to the scenario, and predicting the outcome. Use physics vocabulary precisely.
  • Energy conservation: Always identify all forms of energy at initial and final states. ΔKE + ΔPE + ΔE_thermal = W_external. If the system is isolated, total mechanical energy is conserved only if no non-conservative forces act.
  • Circuit analysis: In series: current is the same everywhere, voltages add. In parallel: voltage is the same across branches, currents add. Use these rules to identify what changes when a bulb burns out.

📋 How FRQ Scoring Works

Section I (50%): 50 MCQs in 90 min (includes multi-select questions: select ALL correct answers). Section II (50%): 5 FRQs in 90 min.

FRQ types and scoring:
• Experimental Design (~12 pts): Design an experiment, identify measurements, explain data analysis method, and address sources of error. Each element = points.
• Qualitative/Quantitative Translation (~12 pts): Convert between verbal description, diagram, graph, and equation representations of the same physical situation.
• Short Answer (3-4 pts each): Apply physics principles to a specific scenario. Must show work and explain reasoning.
• Paragraph Argument (4 pts): Write 3 to 5 sentences explaining a physics phenomenon. Must use physics vocabulary precisely and apply the correct principle to the scenario.

Scoring principle: Partial credit for correct physics even if math contains errors. Free-body diagrams earn points even if F=ma calculation is wrong. Always draw a labeled FBD before calculating.

Formula sheet: Provided. Know what's on it so you use exam time efficiently, not searching.

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. Newton's Second Law (F = ma) states that:

  • A. Force equals mass times velocity
  • B. The net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration
  • C. Action equals reaction
  • D. Every object remains at rest unless acted upon by a net force
Show Answer

Answer: B: Newton's 2nd law: the net force on an object equals the product of its mass and acceleration. It defines the relationship between force, mass, and the resulting motion change.

Q2. A block slides down a frictionless inclined plane at angle θ. The acceleration of the block is:

  • A. g (gravitational acceleration)
  • B. g sin θ
  • C. g cos θ
  • D. g tan θ
Show Answer

Answer: B: On a frictionless incline, the component of gravity along the incline = mg sin θ. By Newton's 2nd law: ma = mg sin θ → a = g sin θ.

Q3. Conservation of momentum states that in a closed system:

  • A. Kinetic energy is always conserved
  • B. The total momentum of all objects remains constant if no external net force acts on the system
  • C. Only elastic collisions conserve momentum
  • D. Momentum is conserved only in collisions
Show Answer

Answer: B: Conservation of linear momentum: in the absence of external net force, the total momentum (p = mv) of a system remains constant. This applies to all collisions elastic and inelastic.

Q4. Which of the following quantities is a vector?

  • A. Speed
  • B. Velocity
  • C. Kinetic energy
  • D. Mass
Show Answer

Answer: B: Velocity is a vector (magnitude + direction). Speed is a scalar (magnitude only). Kinetic energy and mass are scalars.

Q5. The work done by a constant force F on an object displaced distance d at angle θ to the force is:

  • A. F × d
  • B. F × d × cos θ
  • C. F × d × sin θ
  • D. F / d
Show Answer

Answer: B: Work = F, d, cos θ. Only the component of force parallel to the displacement does work. If the force is perpendicular to displacement, cos 90° = 0 and no work is done.

Q6. For uniform circular motion, the centripetal acceleration is directed:

  • A. Tangentially in the direction of motion
  • B. Toward the center of the circle
  • C. Away from the center of the circle
  • D. Vertically downward
Show Answer

Answer: B: Centripetal acceleration (ac = v²/r) is always directed toward the center of the circular path it changes the direction of velocity without changing its magnitude.

Q7. The relationship between electric potential energy (U), charge (q), and electric potential (V) is:

  • A. U = q/V
  • B. U = qV
  • C. U = V/q
  • D. U = q²V
Show Answer

Answer: B: Electric potential energy U = qV. The electric potential V is the potential energy per unit charge; multiplying by charge q gives the total potential energy of the charge in the field.

Q8. Ohm's Law states that:

  • A. Power equals current times voltage
  • B. Voltage equals current times resistance (V = IR), for ohmic conductors
  • C. Current is inversely proportional to resistance only
  • D. Resistance increases with current
Show Answer

Answer: B: Ohm's Law: V = IR. For ohmic materials, voltage across a conductor is proportional to current flowing through it, with resistance R as the proportionality constant.

Q9. In a simple series circuit with two resistors R1 and R2, the equivalent resistance is:

  • A. R1 × R2 / (R1 + R2)
  • B. R1 + R2
  • C. 1/(1/R1 + 1/R2)
  • D. R1 - R2
Show Answer

Answer: B: Series circuit: resistors add directly. Total resistance = R1 + R2 + ... + Rn. In parallel, the reciprocal of total resistance equals the sum of reciprocals.

Q10. Which of the following correctly describes a standing wave?

  • A. A wave that travels only in one direction
  • B. A wave pattern formed by the superposition of two identical waves traveling in opposite directions
  • C. A wave that doesn't transfer energy
  • D. A wave with infinite wavelength
Show Answer

Answer: B: Standing waves form when two identical waves (same frequency, amplitude) traveling in opposite directions interfere. The result is fixed nodes (zero amplitude) and antinodes (maximum amplitude) at characteristic positions.

Q11. The Doppler effect predicts that when a wave source moves toward a stationary observer:

  • A. The observed frequency decreases
  • B. The observed frequency increases compared to the emitted frequency
  • C. The speed of the wave increases
  • D. The wavelength of the wave increases
Show Answer

Answer: B: Doppler effect: when source approaches observer, successive wavefronts are compressed (shorter wavelength), increasing the observed frequency. When source recedes, frequency decreases.

Q12. According to Archimedes' principle, the buoyant force on an object is equal to:

  • A. The weight of the object itself
  • B. The weight of the fluid displaced by the object
  • C. The pressure times the object's surface area
  • D. The density of the object times gravity
Show Answer

Answer: B: Archimedes' principle: buoyant force = weight of fluid displaced = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g. Objects float when buoyant force equals their weight.

Q13. The electric field between two parallel charged plates with charge density σ is:

  • A. σ/ε₀ outside the plates
  • B. σ/ε₀ between the plates, directed from positive to negative plate
  • C. σ²/ε₀
  • D. Zero between the plates
Show Answer

Answer: B: For infinite parallel plate capacitor: E = σ/ε₀ between the plates, directed from positive to negative plate. This uniform field is useful for particle accelerators and capacitors.

Q14. The period of a simple pendulum (for small angles) depends primarily on:

  • A. The mass of the bob
  • B. The length of the pendulum and gravitational acceleration (T = 2π√(L/g))
  • C. The amplitude of swing
  • D. Both length and mass
Show Answer

Answer: B: Simple pendulum period T = 2π√(L/g) independent of mass and amplitude (for small angles). This is why pendulum clocks work: the period is determined entirely by length and local g.

Q15. In the photoelectric effect, the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons depends on:

  • A. The intensity of incident light
  • B. The frequency of incident light minus the work function of the metal
  • C. The number of photons per second
  • D. The wavelength of light only
Show Answer

Answer: B: Photoelectric equation (Einstein): KE_max = hf - φ (work function). KE depends on photon frequency, not intensity. Intensity affects the number of electrons emitted, not their energy this was inexplicable by classical physics.

Q16. Which type of electromagnetic radiation has the shortest wavelength?

  • A. Radio waves
  • B. Gamma rays
  • C. Visible light
  • D. Infrared radiation
Show Answer

Answer: B: The EM spectrum in order of increasing wavelength (decreasing frequency/energy): gamma rays, X-rays, UV, visible, infrared, microwave, radio. Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength (~10⁻¹² m) and highest energy.

Q17. Snell's Law describes:

  • A. The reflection of light off mirrors
  • B. Refraction how light bends when passing from one medium to another (n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2)
  • C. Diffraction of light through a slit
  • D. Dispersion of white light into a spectrum
Show Answer

Answer: B: Snell's Law: n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2. When light enters a denser medium (higher n), it bends toward the normal (θ decreases). When entering a less dense medium, it bends away from normal.

Q18. Conservation of energy states that in a closed system:

  • A. Kinetic energy is always constant
  • B. The total energy (kinetic + potential + other forms) remains constant if only conservative forces act
  • C. Potential energy is always converted to kinetic energy
  • D. Friction increases total mechanical energy
Show Answer

Answer: B: Conservation of mechanical energy: in systems with only conservative forces (gravity, springs), total mechanical energy (KE + PE) is constant. Friction dissipates mechanical energy into thermal energy.

Q19. The magnetic force on a moving charge is given by F = qv × B. The magnetic force on a positive charge moving to the right in a magnetic field pointing out of the page is:

  • A. Upward (out of the page)
  • B. Downward (into the page)
  • C. In the direction of motion
  • D. Upward (in the plane of the page)
Show Answer

Answer: B: Right-hand rule for F = qv × B: fingers point in direction of v (right), curl toward B (out of page) → thumb points upward. For positive charge, force is upward. For the cross product v(rightward) × B(out of page): rightward × out-of-page = downward direction.

Q20. The second law of thermodynamics states that:

  • A. Energy can be neither created nor destroyed
  • B. In any natural process, the total entropy of a closed system tends to increase
  • C. Heat always flows from cold to hot
  • D. Work can be completely converted to heat with 100% efficiency
Show Answer

Answer: B: The second law: entropy of an isolated system always increases (or stays constant for reversible processes). Heat spontaneously flows from hot to cold; work can be fully converted to heat but heat cannot be fully converted to work.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (FRQ)

A 2 kg block is pushed along a horizontal surface with a 10 N horizontal force. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and surface is μk = 0.25. (a) Draw a free body diagram showing all forces on the block. (b) Calculate the net force on the block and its acceleration. (c) If the block starts from rest, calculate its velocity after 4 seconds.
View Model Answer
(a) Free Body Diagram: The block has four forces: Weight W = mg = 2 × 10 = 20 N downward; Normal force N = 20 N upward (since no vertical acceleration); Applied force F_app = 10 N to the right (horizontal); Friction force f_k = μk × N = 0.25 × 20 = 5 N to the left (opposing motion). (b) Net force: Apply Newton's Second Law in the horizontal direction: F_net = F_app - f_k = 10 N - 5 N = 5 N (to the right). Acceleration: a = F_net / m = 5 N / 2 kg = 2.5 m/s² (c) Using kinematics (v = v₀ + at): Initial velocity v₀ = 0 (starts from rest). Time t = 4 s. Velocity after 4 s: v = 0 + (2.5 m/s²)(4 s) = 10 m/s

FRQ 2 (FRQ)

A simple pendulum of length 1.5 m is released from an angle of 10° from vertical. (a) Calculate the period of the pendulum. (g = 9.8 m/s²) (b) Calculate the maximum speed of the pendulum bob, using energy conservation. Assume the mass of the bob is 0.5 kg. (c) Explain why the period would be different if this experiment were performed on the Moon.
View Model Answer
(a) Period T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(1.5/9.8) = 2π√(0.153) = 2π(0.391) = 2.46 seconds. The period is independent of the bob's mass and (for small angles) independent of the amplitude of swing. (b) Using conservation of mechanical energy. The height of the pendulum bob above its lowest point: h = L(1 - cos θ) = 1.5(1 - cos 10°) = 1.5(1 - 0.985) = 1.5(0.015) = 0.023 m. At the lowest point, all potential energy converts to kinetic energy: mgh = ½mv². v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 0.023) = √(0.451) = 0.67 m/s (c) The Moon's gravitational acceleration is approximately g_Moon = 1.62 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's). The period formula T = 2π√(L/g) shows that smaller g produces a longer period. On the Moon with the same length pendulum: T_Moon = 2π√(1.5/1.62) = 2π√(0.926) = 2π(0.962) = 6.05 seconds about 2.5 times longer than on Earth. Gravitational acceleration is the restoring force for a pendulum; weaker gravity means weaker restoring force, making the pendulum oscillate more slowly.

FRQ 3 (FRQ)

Two point charges Q1 = +4 μC and Q2 = -2 μC are placed 0.3 m apart. (a) Calculate the electrostatic force between the charges. Is it attractive or repulsive? (b) Explain qualitatively how the electric field between the two charges differs from the field outside them. (c) A third charge Q3 = +1 μC is placed at the midpoint between Q1 and Q2. Calculate the net force on Q3.
View Model Answer
(a) Coulomb's Law: F = kQ1Q2/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(4×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁻⁶)/(0.3)² = (8.99×10⁹)(8×10⁻¹²)/0.09 = 71.92×10⁻³/0.09 = 0.799 N ≈ 0.80 N. The force is ATTRACTIVE because the charges have opposite signs (one positive, one negative). Equal and opposite forces act on each charge Q1 is attracted toward Q2 and Q2 toward Q1. (b) Electric field between the charges: Electric field lines run from positive to negative charges. Between Q1(+) and Q2(-), the fields from both charges point in the SAME direction (from + toward -) both E fields from Q1 and E field from Q2 point in the same direction between the charges, so they add together and the field between the charges is stronger. Outside the charges, on the far side of each charge, the two field contributions partially cancel (they point in opposite directions), making the field weaker. The field between the charges is stronger than the field outside, and the field lines are more concentrated between the charges. c) Position of Q3: at the midpoint, r = 0.15 m from each charge. Force from Q1 (+4 μC) on Q3 (+1 μC): F1 = k(Q1)(Q3)/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(4×10⁻⁶)(1×10⁻⁶)/(0.15)² = 35.96×10⁻³/0.0225 = 1.60 N. Direction: repulsive Q3 pushed away from Q1 (toward Q2). Force from Q2 (-2 μC) on Q3 (+1 μC): F2 = k(Q2)(Q3)/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)(1×10⁻⁶)/(0.15)² = 0.80 N. Direction: attractive Q3 pulled toward Q2. Both forces act in the same direction (from Q1 toward Q2). Net force = F1 + F2 = 1.60 + 0.80 = 2.40 N directed toward Q2.

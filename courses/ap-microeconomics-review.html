How to Get a 5 on This Exam

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

AP Micro focuses on supply and demand, elasticity, consumer and producer surplus, firm behavior across market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly), factor markets, market failures (externalities, public goods), and government intervention. Graphs are central market structure graphs (MR=MC rule, profit rectangles, deadweight loss triangles) are tested extensively.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Students confuse the profit-maximizing rule (MR=MC) with allocative efficiency (P=MC). In monopoly graphs, students often draw ATC incorrectly or fail to show the profit rectangle properly. Students confuse elastic vs. inelastic demand effects on total revenue if demand is inelastic, raising price increases total revenue. Students also conflate shifts in supply/demand with movements along curves.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

A 3 student knows definitions. A 5 student can draw any market structure graph from memory, identify efficiency conditions, and explain government interventions. Given a scenario like 'a negative externality in production,' a 5 student immediately draws the market, shows MSC above MPC, identifies overproduction and deadweight loss, and explains the Pigouvian tax solution all in one integrated response.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Master the four market structure graphs. Practice drawing them: find profit-maximizing output (MR=MC), find price on demand curve, find ATC at that output, shade profit or loss rectangle, and identify deadweight loss where applicable. Do externality graphs (positive and negative, consumption and production). Practice consumer/producer surplus calculations.

Exam-Day Skills

  • Market structure checklist: For each market structure, know: number of firms, product differentiation, barriers to entry, short-run profit/loss possibility, long-run equilibrium condition, and efficiency (allocative, productive, neither).
  • MR=MC rule: This rule determines the quantity in ALL market structures. Never set P=MC for monopoly that's competitive efficiency, not profit maximization.
  • Elasticity and total revenue: Draw a quick demand curve and test whether TR rises or falls when you increase price. If demand is inelastic (steep), TR rises with price. If elastic (flat), TR falls.
  • Graph labeling: Every microeconomics graph needs: labeled axes (Price/Quantity), demand curve (D), supply curve (S), MR curve for non-competitive markets, ATC curve, and equilibrium point labeled (P*, Q*).
  • Deadweight loss triangles: DWL always represents transactions that are mutually beneficial but don't occur. In monopoly, it's between Q_monopoly and Q_competitive. In negative externality, it's between Q_market and Q_optimal.

📋 How FRQ Scoring Works

Section I (66%): 60 MCQs in 70 min. Section II (34%): 3 FRQs in 60 min (1 long + 2 short).

Long FRQ (~10 pts): Usually involves drawing a market structure graph (perfect competition, monopoly, or monopolistic competition), identifying profit-maximizing output and price, showing profit/loss, and analyzing efficiency or government intervention.

Short FRQs (~5 pts each): May cover consumer/producer surplus, elasticity, externalities, factor markets, or comparative advantage. Each part typically requires identifying a direction, drawing a graph, or explaining a mechanism.

Graph scoring: For monopoly FRQs: correctly drawn demand (D), marginal revenue (MR, below D), average total cost (ATC), marginal cost (MC). Profit-maximizing output where MR=MC (vertical line up to D for price). Profit rectangle: between P and ATC at profit-maximizing Q. Each element = 1 pt.

Verbal explanations: For 'explain' questions, use the following structure: state the direction of change, identify the cause, and explain the economic mechanism. 'Price rises because the decrease in supply creates excess demand at the original price, causing buyers to bid up the price.'

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. The law of demand states that, holding other things equal:

  • A. Higher prices lead to higher quantity demanded
  • B. There is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded
  • C. Demand increases when supply decreases
  • D. Consumer income determines demand
Show Answer

Answer: B: The law of demand: as price rises, quantity demanded falls (and vice versa), reflecting diminishing marginal utility and substitution effects.

Q2. When marginal cost equals marginal revenue for a perfectly competitive firm:

  • A. The firm should shut down
  • B. The firm is maximizing profit
  • C. The firm is operating at minimum average total cost
  • D. The firm earns zero economic profit
Show Answer

Answer: B: The profit-maximization condition for all firms: produce where MC = MR. For perfectly competitive firms, MR = P, so produce where MC = P.

Q3. A negative externality occurs when:

  • A. A firm's production costs exceed its revenues
  • B. A market transaction imposes costs on third parties not reflected in the market price
  • C. Government intervention distorts market prices
  • D. Consumers receive positive spillover benefits
Show Answer

Answer: B: Negative externalities (e.g., pollution): social cost exceeds private cost. The market overproduces relative to the socially optimal level because producers don't bear the full cost of their actions.

Q4. Price discrimination requires:

  • A. A perfectly competitive market
  • B. Market power and the ability to separate customers by willingness to pay
  • C. Perfect information for all buyers
  • D. Government authorization
Show Answer

Answer: B: Price discrimination requires: (1) market power (price-setting ability), (2) ability to identify and separate customers with different elasticities/willingness to pay, and (3) no arbitrage between groups.

Q5. An increase in the price of a complement good will most likely:

  • A. Increase demand for the original good
  • B. Decrease demand for the original good
  • C. Not affect demand for the original good
  • D. Increase supply of the original good
Show Answer

Answer: B: Complements are goods consumed together (cars and gasoline). If gasoline prices rise, car demand falls consuming one requires consuming the other, so a price increase for one reduces demand for both.

Q6. The 'invisible hand' metaphor (Adam Smith) describes:

  • A. Government intervention directing market outcomes
  • B. How self-interested individual decisions in competitive markets can produce socially efficient outcomes without central direction
  • C. The hidden costs of monopoly
  • D. Regulatory capture of government agencies
Show Answer

Answer: B: Smith's invisible hand: when competitive markets function well, self-interested decisions by producers (seeking profit) and consumers (seeking value) coordinate economic activity efficiently without central planning.

Q7. Consumer surplus is defined as:

  • A. The difference between total revenue and total cost for producers
  • B. The difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay
  • C. The profit earned by firms in competitive markets
  • D. The tax revenue government collects from sales
Show Answer

Answer: B: Consumer surplus = willingness to pay - actual price paid. It is the 'bonus' consumers receive by paying less than their maximum willingness to pay.

Q8. Which market structure features many sellers, differentiated products, and easy entry?

  • A. Perfect competition
  • B. Monopolistic competition
  • C. Oligopoly
  • D. Monopoly
Show Answer

Answer: B: Monopolistic competition: many firms sell differentiated (but substitutable) products, have some pricing power, but easy entry erodes long-run economic profits.

Q9. The price elasticity of demand measures:

  • A. How supply changes in response to price changes
  • B. How responsive quantity demanded is to changes in price
  • C. The relationship between income and demand
  • D. The effect of advertising on demand
Show Answer

Answer: B: PED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price. Values > 1 indicate elastic demand; < 1 indicate inelastic demand.

Q10. A monopolist maximizes profit by producing where:

  • A. Price equals marginal cost
  • B. Marginal revenue equals marginal cost
  • C. Average total cost is minimized
  • D. Price equals average variable cost
Show Answer

Answer: B: Like all firms, monopolists maximize profit where MR = MC. Unlike competitive firms, monopolists face downward-sloping demand, so MR < P, meaning they produce less and charge more than the competitive outcome.

Q11. Diminishing marginal returns occur when:

  • A. Total output begins to decrease
  • B. Each additional unit of a variable input adds less to total output than the previous unit
  • C. Fixed costs increase with output
  • D. Price falls below average variable cost
Show Answer

Answer: B: Diminishing marginal returns: as more of a variable input (labor) is added to fixed inputs (capital), the marginal product of the variable input eventually decreases.

Q12. Which of the following represents a barrier to entry in a monopoly?

  • A. Presence of many substitute products
  • B. Patent protection, control of key resources, or government licensing
  • C. Presence of many competitors
  • D. Low fixed costs in the industry
Show Answer

Answer: B: Barriers to entry (patents, resource control, network effects, government licensing) prevent competition from eroding monopoly profits in the long run.

Q13. The Coase Theorem states that:

  • A. Government should tax all negative externalities
  • B. When transaction costs are low, private bargaining can achieve efficient outcomes even in the presence of externalities
  • C. Markets always fail in the presence of externalities
  • D. Only government regulation can correct externality problems
Show Answer

Answer: B: Coase: if property rights are clearly defined and transaction costs are low, affected parties will bargain to the efficient outcome regardless of who initially holds property rights.

Q14. Deadweight loss in a monopoly occurs because:

  • A. The monopolist earns profit
  • B. The monopolist produces less than the socially optimal output, causing mutually beneficial transactions not to occur
  • C. Production costs are higher in monopoly
  • D. The monopolist charges the same price to all consumers
Show Answer

Answer: B: Deadweight loss: the monopolist restricts output below the competitive level (where MC = P), so some consumers whose WTP exceeds MC don't buy destroying potential gains from trade.

Q15. A perfectly inelastic demand curve is:

  • A. Horizontal
  • B. Vertical
  • C. Upward sloping
  • D. Unit elastic
Show Answer

Answer: B: Perfectly inelastic demand (PED = 0): quantity demanded doesn't change regardless of price represented by a vertical demand curve. Common for necessities with no substitutes (e.g., insulin).

Q16. A Pigouvian tax is designed to:

  • A. Raise government revenue efficiently
  • B. Correct a negative externality by making producers internalize the social cost of their production
  • C. Redistribute income from rich to poor
  • D. Subsidize the production of public goods
Show Answer

Answer: B: A Pigouvian tax equal to the marginal external cost shifts the supply curve leftward until the market equilibrium equals the socially optimal quantity, internalizing the externality.

Q17. In the long run for a perfectly competitive firm:

  • A. Economic profit is positive
  • B. Economic profit is zero (only normal/accounting profit remains)
  • C. Economic profit is negative
  • D. Economic profit equals average total cost
Show Answer

Answer: B: In long-run perfect competition, entry of new firms when profit is positive and exit when profit is negative drives economic profit to zero. Price = minimum ATC.

Q18. The concept of 'opportunity cost' is best defined as:

  • A. The monetary cost of a purchase
  • B. The value of the next-best alternative foregone when making a decision
  • C. The explicit cost of a production decision
  • D. The difference between total and marginal cost
Show Answer

Answer: B: Opportunity cost is the fundamental economic concept: every choice has a cost equal to the best alternative given up. It includes both explicit costs and implicit costs (foregone income/value).

Q19. Which of the following is an example of a public good?

  • A. A movie streaming service
  • B. National defense
  • C. A toll road
  • D. A private park with admission charge
Show Answer

Answer: B: Public goods are non-rival (consumption by one doesn't reduce availability to others) and non-excludable (can't prevent people from using them). National defense is the classic example.

Q20. Factor markets differ from product markets in that:

  • A. Factor markets are always competitive
  • B. Demand for factors of production is derived from demand for the goods they produce
  • C. Factor markets have no equilibrium
  • D. Firms are buyers in product markets and sellers in factor markets
Show Answer

Answer: B: Derived demand: firms demand labor, capital, and other inputs not for their own sake but to produce goods for sale. The demand for factors depends on the demand for the products they produce.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (FRQ)

A profit-maximizing firm operates in a perfectly competitive market. The market price is $20. The firm's costs are: Q=1: TC=$30 | Q=2: TC=$38 | Q=3: TC=$48 | Q=4: TC=$60 | Q=5: TC=$75 | Q=6: TC=$96 a) Calculate the marginal cost at each level of output from Q=2 to Q=6. b) Determine the profit-maximizing output level and calculate the firm's profit or loss. c) In the long run, what will happen to this firm and to the market if many firms in this market have the same cost structure?
View Model Answer
a) Marginal Cost Calculations (MC = change in TC / change in Q): MC at Q=2: $38-$30 = $8 MC at Q=3: $48-$38 = $10 MC at Q=4: $60-$48 = $12 MC at Q=5: $75-$60 = $15 MC at Q=6: $96-$75 = $21 b) Profit-maximizing output: The firm produces where MC = MR = P = $20. At Q=5: MC = $15 < $20 (keep producing). At Q=6: MC = $21 > $20 (stop). Therefore, the profit-maximizing output is Q=5. Profit = Total Revenue - Total Cost = (P × Q) - TC = ($20 × 5) - $75 = $100 - $75 = $25 economic profit. c) In the long run, positive economic profit ($25) attracts new firms to enter this perfectly competitive market. New entry increases market supply, driving the market price downward. Existing firms see their revenue decline while costs remain the same, reducing profit. Entry continues until economic profit is eliminated price falls to the minimum average total cost. At long-run equilibrium: P = MC = minimum ATC, economic profit = 0. All firms earn only normal (accounting) profit, and the number of firms in the market increases until the market clears at this new lower price.

FRQ 2 (FRQ)

a) Explain why a natural monopoly exists and give ONE example. b) Using a cost-revenue diagram, explain why an unregulated natural monopoly produces a deadweight loss. c) Explain TWO methods governments use to regulate natural monopolies and one problem with each method.
View Model Answer
a) A natural monopoly exists when a single firm can produce the entire market output at lower cost than multiple firms competing. This occurs when fixed costs are extremely high relative to variable costs once the massive infrastructure is built (railway lines, power grids, water pipes), the marginal cost of serving additional customers is very low. The long-run average total cost curve declines throughout the relevant range of demand. A classic example is a regional electric utility: it would be wasteful to have multiple companies each build separate power grids serving the same area; one grid serving all customers is far more cost-efficient. b) Diagram Description: Draw a downward-sloping demand curve and downward-sloping LRATC curve that lies below demand in the relevant range. MR lies below demand (as for all monopolists). Unregulated monopoly: firm sets MR=MC (where MC is the nearly flat marginal cost), producing Qm and charging Pm (found on demand curve). Deadweight loss is the triangle between Qm and the efficient quantity Qeff (where demand = MC), representing transactions that would benefit both parties but don't occur because the monopolist restricts output. c) Method 1: Marginal cost pricing regulation: Government requires the firm to set P = MC. Problem: For a natural monopoly, MC < LRATC throughout (that's why it's a natural monopoly), so P = MC means P < ATC, producing a loss. The firm would exit without a government subsidy, creating a government expenditure burden and potentially distorting tax incentives. Method 2: Rate-of-return regulation (average cost pricing): Government allows the firm to charge P = ATC, ensuring it earns zero economic profit (normal return on investment). Problem: This eliminates the incentive to minimize costs if the firm is guaranteed a return on its costs, it has no incentive to be efficient. This 'gold-plating' problem (Averch-Johnson effect) leads regulated firms to over-invest in capital and pad costs, since they are permitted to earn a return on all assets.

FRQ 3 (FRQ)

a) Explain the difference between accounting profit and economic profit. b) A firm has total revenues of $500,000, explicit costs of $350,000, and an owner's foregone salary of $80,000. Calculate accounting profit and economic profit. c) Why might a firm with positive accounting profit still decide to exit an industry in the long run?
View Model Answer
a) Accounting profit = Total Revenue - Explicit Costs (out-of-pocket expenses like wages, rent, materials, utilities). It is the profit reported in financial statements for tax purposes. Economic profit = Total Revenue - Total Costs (explicit costs + implicit costs). Implicit costs include the opportunity costs of owner-provided resources: the owner's foregone salary if they could work elsewhere, foregone return on owner-invested capital, and foregone rent if the owner uses their own property. Economic profit represents whether the firm is doing better than its next-best alternative use of resources it is the relevant concept for long-run entry and exit decisions. b) Accounting Profit = Total Revenue - Explicit Costs = $500,000 - $350,000 = $150,000. Economic Profit = Total Revenue - Explicit Costs - Implicit Costs = $500,000 - $350,000 - $80,000 = $70,000. The owner's $80,000 foregone salary is an implicit cost that accounting profit ignores but economic profit includes. c) A firm with positive accounting profit might exit if its economic profit is negative or zero. If the owner could earn more by working elsewhere (higher opportunity cost), the firm is not covering all its economic costs even while showing accounting profit. In perfectly competitive markets, long-run equilibrium requires economic profit = 0 (P = minimum ATC). If a firm earns positive accounting profit but negative economic profit, it means resources are more valuable in alternative uses. Rational owners exit to pursue better opportunities, even if their accountant shows the business is 'profitable.'

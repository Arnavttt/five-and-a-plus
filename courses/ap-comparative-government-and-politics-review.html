How to Get a 5 on This Exam

📊 What the Exam Tests Most

AP Comp Gov tests six countries (UK, Russia, China, Iran, Mexico, Nigeria) across six themes: sovereignty and legitimacy, political institutions, political culture, political participation, civil society, and international relations. FRQs are 50% of the score and require comparing two or more countries using political science concepts. Conceptual vocabulary (authoritarianism, federalism, corporatism, rentier state) is heavily tested.

⚠️ Where Students Lose Points

Students describe countries without using comparative political science concepts. Saying 'China has a one-party system' earns less than 'China's CCP maintains hegemonic party control through nomenklatura appointments, eliminating meaningful electoral competition.' Students also fail to make comparative claims 'both' and 'whereas' are your best friends.

🎯 What Separates a 3 from a 5

A 3 student knows facts about each country. A 5 student uses country examples to illustrate comparative concepts they can argue that 'semi-presidential systems produce executive power-sharing under cohabitation, as seen in France and theoretically applicable to Russia's constitutional design.' Pattern recognition across countries separates average from excellent responses.

📅 Final 2 to 3 Weeks Strategy

Create a comparison matrix for all 6 countries across: regime type, electoral system, executive structure, federalism, civil society freedom, and economic system. Practice the FRQ types: Conceptual Analysis, Country Context, Comparison, and Country Application. Know 2 to 3 specific examples per country for each major concept.

Exam-Day Skills

  • FRQ concept definition: Always define the political science concept before applying it. 'Federalism, the constitutional division of power between central and subnational governments, is illustrated by Nigeria's 36 states...'
  • Comparison FRQ: Use explicit comparative language 'Unlike the UK's Westminster model where the executive dominates parliament, Russia's system formally separates powers but in practice concentrates authority in the presidency.'
  • MCQ elimination: Wrong answers often swap country characteristics. If an answer describes Nigeria's characteristics but attributes them to China, eliminate it.
  • Country-specific examples: The Duma, Majles, National People's Congress, House of Commons, Chamber of Deputies know each legislature's actual powers and constraints.
  • Current events context: The exam uses real political events as stimuli. Know recent elections, leadership transitions, and protests in all 6 countries through the most recent academic year.

📋 How FRQ Scoring Works

Section I (50%): 55 MCQs in 45 min. Section II (50%): 4 FRQs in 100 min (~25 min each).

FRQ 1: Conceptual Analysis (5 pts): Defines a concept, applies it to two of the six required countries, and explains a similarity/difference between those two countries. Uses political science concepts as the organizing framework: NOT just listing country facts.

FRQ 2: Country Context (5 pts): Applies a concept to one country (UK, Russia, China, Iran, Mexico, or Nigeria) using specific evidence. Must explain how the political context shapes the concept's application in that country.

FRQ 3: Comparison (5 pts): Compares two countries on a specific political concept. Must make explicit comparative claims (similarities AND differences), not just describe each country separately.

FRQ 4: Country Application (5 pts): Uses a stimulus (map, graph, political scenario) and applies a political concept to one or more countries.

Key: Define concepts before applying them. Use precise comparative language ('whereas,' 'in contrast to,' 'similarly'). Always connect country examples to the broader concept being tested.

Practice Questions

Multiple Choice (20 Questions)

Q1. A presidential system differs from a parliamentary system primarily in that:

  • A. Presidential systems have stronger legislatures
  • B. In presidential systems, the executive is directly elected and independent from the legislature
  • C. Parliamentary systems have weaker prime ministers
  • D. Presidential systems always have federal structures
Show Answer

Answer: B: In presidential systems (US, Mexico, Nigeria), the executive is directly elected and serves a fixed term independent of legislative confidence.

Q2. The concept of 'regime type' refers to:

  • A. A country's economic system
  • B. The fundamental rules determining who holds power and how it is exercised
  • C. Electoral systems specifically
  • D. Constitutional court design
Show Answer

Answer: B: Regime type classifies political systems by how power is organized and legitimated: democracy, authoritarianism, totalitarianism.

Q3. China's political system is best classified as:

  • A. A competitive authoritarian regime
  • B. A one-party state with Leninist party organization controlling all major institutions
  • C. A federal democracy
  • D. A theocracy
Show Answer

Answer: B: China is governed by the CCP through a Leninist organizational structure: democratic centralism, party cells in all organizations, and nomenklatura control of all major appointments.

Q4. The United Kingdom's 'unwritten constitution' means:

  • A. Britain has no constitutional principles
  • B. Constitutional rules are found in statutes, conventions, and common law rather than a single codified document
  • C. Parliament is unconstrained by any constitutional limits
  • D. Courts cannot review parliamentary acts
Show Answer

Answer: B: Britain's constitution is 'uncodified' comprising the Magna Carta, Parliament Acts, Human Rights Act, conventions, and common law.

Q5. Mexico's transition to competitive democracy was most clearly marked by:

  • A. The 1917 Constitution
  • B. The 2000 presidential election won by Vicente Fox (PAN), ending 71 years of PRI rule
  • C. NAFTA implementation in 1994
  • D. The Zapatista uprising
Show Answer

Answer: B: Fox's 2000 victory represented Mexico's first peaceful transfer of power to an opposition party genuine electoral competition had arrived.

Q6. Nigeria's federal structure was designed primarily to:

  • A. Centralize power in Lagos
  • B. Accommodate ethnic and regional diversity by distributing power among states
  • C. Follow British colonial precedent exactly
  • D. Ensure military control of government
Show Answer

Answer: B: Nigeria's 36-state federal structure explicitly addresses its ~250 ethnic groups by distributing political and economic power.

Q7. Russia under Putin is best classified as:

  • A. A liberal democracy
  • B. A competitive authoritarian regime where elections occur but the playing field is severely unequal
  • C. A totalitarian state
  • D. A military dictatorship
Show Answer

Answer: B: Russia holds elections, but opposition faces legal harassment, media is controlled, and incumbents use state resources unfairly elections legitimate rule without enabling genuine competition.

Q8. Iran's political system is unique because it combines:

  • A. Democracy and theocracy in a dual sovereignty structure
  • B. Military and clerical rule
  • C. Monarchy and republicanism
  • D. Federalism and unitarianism
Show Answer

Answer: A: Iran's Islamic Republic overlays a theocratic Guardian Council and Supreme Leader over elected republican institutions (president, parliament).

Q9. 'State capacity' refers to:

  • A. A country's military strength
  • B. The ability of state institutions to implement policy and enforce law throughout the territory
  • C. The size of a country's bureaucracy
  • D. Economic development level
Show Answer

Answer: B: State capacity measures whether governments can actually do what they decide to do collect taxes, deliver services, enforce contracts, and maintain a monopoly on legitimate violence.

Q10. A unitary state differs from a federal state in that:

  • A. Unitary states have stronger militaries
  • B. In unitary states, all sub-national units derive authority from the central government, which can alter or abolish them
  • C. Unitary states have more elections
  • D. Federal states have presidents, unitary states have prime ministers
Show Answer

Answer: B: In unitary systems (UK, France, China, Iran), regional governments exist at the pleasure of the center. In federal systems, sub-national units have constitutionally guaranteed autonomy.

Q11. The Chinese National People's Congress is best described as:

  • A. An independent legislative body checking CCP power
  • B. A rubber-stamp institution that formally ratifies CCP decisions rather than independently legislating
  • C. An advisory council of technocrats
  • D. China's equivalent of the US Senate
Show Answer

Answer: B: The NPC meets briefly, passes virtually all proposals unanimously, and serves primarily to legitimate party decisions. Real power flows through CCP Politburo Standing Committee channels.

Q12. 'Legitimacy' in political science refers to:

  • A. Legal authority derived from constitution
  • B. The belief among the governed that a regime has the right to rule
  • C. Military power to enforce compliance
  • D. International recognition of a government
Show Answer

Answer: B: Weber's legitimacy framework: the belief that power is rightfully held and exercised without which coercion alone cannot sustain governance.

Q13. Proportional representation systems tend to produce:

  • A. Two-party systems
  • B. Multiparty systems with coalition governments
  • C. One-party dominance
  • D. Military control of government
Show Answer

Answer: B: PR systems translate vote shares into seat shares, lowering the threshold for small parties encouraging more parties and typically producing fragmented legislatures requiring coalitions.

Q14. The 'resource curse' describes:

  • A. Oil-rich countries always developing democracies
  • B. Countries with abundant natural resources often experiencing slower development and democratic stagnation
  • C. Natural resources automatically causing civil war
  • D. Resource-poor countries being unable to develop
Show Answer

Answer: B: Abundant natural resources (especially oil) can undermine development by reducing government accountability, enabling repression, causing Dutch disease, and funding corruption.

Q15. Corporatism differs from pluralism in that:

  • A. Corporatism allows more interest groups
  • B. The state officially recognizes and grants monopoly representation to a limited number of peak associations, integrating them into policymaking
  • C. Corporatism is exclusively found in democracies
  • D. Pluralism involves government-controlled groups
Show Answer

Answer: B: Corporatism designates official peak associations for labor, capital, and agriculture to negotiate policy with the state. Pluralism (US model) allows unlimited competing groups with no state monopoly recognition.

Q16. The EU is considered a supranational organization because:

  • A. Its member states share a language
  • B. Member states transfer sovereignty in specified areas to EU institutions whose decisions are binding on members
  • C. The EU has its own military
  • D. All EU decisions require unanimous consent
Show Answer

Answer: B: EU law is supreme over member state law in its domains, and EU institutions can make binding decisions even against member state preferences.

Q17. Cleavage theory holds that:

  • A. All democracies will eventually fail
  • B. Social divisions (class, religion, ethnicity, region) shape party systems and political competition
  • C. Economic inequality causes revolution
  • D. Electoral systems determine policy outcomes
Show Answer

Answer: B: Lipset and Rokkan: party systems reflect historically frozen social divisions mapping onto industrial, religious, center-periphery, and rural-urban cleavages.

Q18. The Gini coefficient measures:

  • A. A country's GDP growth rate
  • B. Economic inequality with 0 representing perfect equality and 1 representing maximum inequality
  • C. Electoral system proportionality
  • D. Level of democratic freedom
Show Answer

Answer: B: The Gini coefficient quantifies income distribution inequality. South Africa (0.63) is among the world's most unequal; Scandinavian countries (0.25-0.28) the most equal.

Q19. Semi-presidentialism (as in France) differs from pure presidentialism in that:

  • A. There is no legislature
  • B. Both a directly elected president and a prime minister responsible to parliament exercise executive power
  • C. The president is elected by parliament
  • D. There is no prime minister
Show Answer

Answer: B: French Fifth Republic semi-presidentialism has both a president (controls foreign/defense policy) and a prime minister (responsible to the National Assembly). During 'cohabitation,' they come from different parties.

Q20. Which best explains the persistence of authoritarian regimes despite economic modernization?

  • A. Economic development never challenges authoritarianism
  • B. Authoritarian regimes adapt through selective liberalization, co-opting elites, and using nationalist appeals to maintain legitimacy
  • C. Only military intervention causes democratization
  • D. Economic development automatically produces democracy
Show Answer

Answer: B: Regimes like China and Singapore show that economic development with authoritarian governance is sustainable through elite co-optation, nationalist legitimacy, welfare delivery, and repression of organized opposition.

Free Response Questions

FRQ 1 (Country Comparison FRQ)

Compare the role of elections in the United Kingdom and in Russia. a) Describe how elections in the United Kingdom reflect democratic principles. b) Describe how elections in Russia differ from those of the United Kingdom. c) Explain why Russia holds elections despite not allowing genuine democratic competition.
View Model Answer
a) UK elections reflect democratic principles through genuine electoral competition: Conservative, Labour, Liberal Democrat, and other parties compete with meaningful uncertainty of outcome, and governing parties have lost elections and peacefully transferred power (1997, 2010, 2024). Elections are administered by an independent Electoral Commission. The press is substantially free, allowing opposition platforms to reach voters. Civil liberties including free speech and assembly enable genuine campaign competition. b) Russian elections differ fundamentally. Opposition figures face systematic harassment: Alexei Navalny was imprisoned and died in custody; other candidates are disqualified on technicalities. State media (RT, Rossiya) provides near-universal favorable coverage of Putin while denying opposition access. United Russia uses state resources for campaigning that independent candidates cannot access. Independent election monitoring is restricted. Putin received 87% in the 2024 election producing predetermined outcomes. c) Russia holds elections because elections serve legitimizing functions even without genuine competition. High reported vote totals signal mass support to potential elite challengers, discouraging coup attempts. Elections provide international legitimacy Russia claims to be a democracy. Elections help identify and reward loyal elites through guaranteed victories. Levitsky and Way's competitive authoritarianism framework suggests elections also serve as mechanisms for gauging relative popularity across regions and mobilizing the state apparatus.

FRQ 2 (Conceptual Analysis FRQ)

a) Define 'civil society' as used in comparative politics. b) Explain how a strong civil society contributes to democratic consolidation using ONE specific country example. c) Explain how authoritarian regimes attempt to control civil society.
View Model Answer
a) Civil society refers to the sphere of organized social life that is voluntary, self-generating, self-supporting, and autonomous from the state. It includes NGOs, trade unions, religious organizations, professional associations, community groups, and independent media occupying the space between private family life and formal state institutions. b) Civil society contributed to Mexican democratic consolidation after 2000. The 1985 Mexico City earthquake sparked autonomous civic organizing as citizens formed their own relief networks, building organizational capacity that persisted. NGOs monitored the 1988 election (revealing massive fraud); civic groups pressured for electoral reforms leading to the independent INE; independent media grew. By 2000, a robust civil society existed capable of credibly monitoring the election in which Fox won civil society's capacity to observe, report, and mobilize made the democratic transition credible and durable. c) Authoritarian regimes control civil society through several strategies. China requires NGOs to register with government ministries and find official government sponsors, giving authorities veto power over organizational existence. Foreign NGO funding is banned or restricted (Foreign NGO Management Law, 2017). The internet is controlled via the 'Great Firewall,' preventing organizing tools from enabling autonomous mobilization. Russia designated many NGOs 'foreign agents' requiring onerous reporting requirements that force closure. The common thread: regimes tolerate civil society filling social welfare gaps while crushing civil society with autonomous political potential.

FRQ 3 (Policy Comparison FRQ)

Compare the policymaking process in China and the United Kingdom. a) Describe ONE way the CCP controls policymaking in China. b) Describe ONE way parliamentary sovereignty shapes policymaking in the UK. c) Explain ONE advantage and ONE disadvantage of the Chinese policymaking model compared to the UK model.
View Model Answer
a) The CCP controls policymaking through the nomenklatura system the party's authority to appoint, promote, and remove all major officials in government, military, state-owned enterprises, universities, and media. Because advancement requires party approval, officials at all levels have strong incentives to implement party policy faithfully. The Politburo Standing Committee (7 members) makes the most consequential decisions in closed deliberations with no formal accountability to any external constituency. b) Parliamentary sovereignty means the UK government (cabinet drawn from Commons majority) can pass virtually any legislation without constitutional veto, provided it maintains its parliamentary majority. A government with a large majority (Blair 1997-2001, Thatcher 1983-87) can implement ambitious policy agendas rapidly. However, the government is always accountable to Parliament losing a confidence vote requires resignation or an election creating ongoing legislative accountability through Question Time and committee scrutiny. c) Advantage: Policy decisions can be implemented rapidly and comprehensively without legislative bargaining delays, judicial review, or electoral cycles. China's infrastructure development, poverty alleviation (lifting 800 million from poverty, 1980-2020), and COVID lockdown implementation demonstrate the model's capacity for decisive large-scale action that pluralist systems struggle to match. Disadvantage: The Chinese model lacks error-correction mechanisms. When policy is wrong Great Leap Forward, Cultural Revolution, initial COVID coverup in Wuhan there are no independent institutions (free press, opposition parties, independent courts) to identify errors and change course before catastrophic damage occurs. The UK's adversarial parliamentary system, free press, and regular elections create feedback mechanisms that, while messy, enable course correction.

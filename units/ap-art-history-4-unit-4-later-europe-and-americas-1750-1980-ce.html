Neoclassicism and the Academy

Neoclassicism (c. 1750 to 1830) revived Greco-Roman subject matter, style, and moral clarity in response to the Rococo's frivolity. Jacques-Louis David's "Oath of the Horatii" (1784) exemplifies this: heroic subject, clarity of line, architectural composition, and moral message tied to republican virtue.

Common misconception: Students equate "neoclassical" with "old-fashioned"; Neoclassicism was politically radical: it opposed the decadent aristocratic Rococo with republican virtue and civic duty.

How it can be tested: Identify key works of Neoclassicism, Romanticism, and Realism; explain their political/social contexts; describe formal differences.

Key Vocabulary
  • Neoclassicism: Late 18th to early 19th century movement reviving Greek and Roman forms, associated with Enlightenment ideals and republican politics
  • Romanticism: Early 19th century movement emphasizing emotion, nature, the sublime, and individualism in reaction to rationalism
  • Realism: Mid-19th century movement depicting everyday working-class life without idealization (Courbet, Daumier)
  • Impressionism: French movement (1860s to 80s) capturing momentary light with loose brushwork and everyday subjects (Monet, Renoir, Degas)
  • chiaroscuro: Strong contrast between light and dark areas, used to create volume and dramatic effect (Caravaggio, Rembrandt)
Objectives
  • Identify formal features of major 19th to 20th century movements
  • Explain how each movement responded to the previous one
  • Analyze a work by Cézanne, Picasso, or Pollock using formal analysis
How this can be tested
  • Compare Monet's garden series with a David Neoclassical work: what changed?
  • Identify the cultural context that produced Cubism
Practice before moving on
  • What political events shaped Neoclassicism and Romanticism?

Q1
The Impressionists were controversial when they first exhibited because
  • A) they depicted only industrial and ugly subjects
  • B) they completely abandoned color in favor of black and white
  • C) they used photography instead of painting
  • D) their loose brushwork, unconventional subjects, and lack of academic finish violated the standards of the Paris Salon
D is correct. their loose brushwork, unconventional subjects, and lack of academic finish violated the standards of the Paris Salon
Q2
Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque developed Cubism (c. 1907 to 1914) to
  • A) produce purely decorative geometric patterns
  • B) return to classical Greek ideals of beauty
  • C) depict objects from multiple viewpoints simultaneously, fragmenting form to show full three-dimensionality on a flat surface
  • D) imitate African masks without any conceptual innovation
C is correct. depict objects from multiple viewpoints simultaneously, fragmenting form to show full three-dimensionality on a flat surface
Q3
Surrealism (founded 1924) drew on __________ to access the unconscious.
  • A) Freudian psychoanalysis and dream imagery, depicting irrational, dreamlike juxtapositions
  • B) Marxist political theory and class struggle
  • C) ancient mythology and religious symbolism
  • D) mathematical theories of space and time
A is correct. Freudian psychoanalysis and dream imagery, depicting irrational, dreamlike juxtapositions
Q4
Abstract Expressionism (1940s to 50s, New York) is characterized by
  • A) geometric abstraction based on mathematical systems
  • B) large-scale canvases, gestural or color-field abstraction, and emphasis on the act of painting as expressive
  • C) precise geometric forms and flat color planes (Minimalism)
  • D) collaged newspaper headlines and commercial imagery (Pop Art)
B is correct. large-scale canvases, gestural or color-field abstraction, and emphasis on the act of painting as expressive
Q5
The advent of photography (1839) affected Western painting by
  • A) freeing painters from the obligation to record reality, contributing to abstraction, Impressionism, and avant-garde experimentation
  • B) eliminating portrait painting completely within 20 years
  • C) having no effect: painters ignored photography entirely
  • D) causing the immediate death of academic painting
A is correct. freeing painters from the obligation to record reality, contributing to abstraction, Impressionism, and avant-garde experimentation
Exit ticket
  • Compare Romanticism's emotion with Neoclassicism's reason
  • Explain why Courbet's Realism was politically controversial
  • How did Cézanne bridge Impressionism and Cubism?

⚡ Common Mistakes & Exam Tips
  • Treating modern art movements as arbitrary rule-breaking rather than reasoned responses to historical conditions (industrialization, WWI trauma, colonialism, photography's challenge to representation).
  • Forgetting that abstraction doesn't mean 'no meaning' abstract works often have precise intellectual or emotional programs. Always ask what the artist intended to express.
  • Exam tip: For Comparison FRQs between modern and pre-modern works, always explain WHY formal choices differ connect the formal difference to the historical/social context of each work.

Q1
Jacques-Louis David's "Oath of the Horatii" (1784) was politically significant because
  • A) it depicted a scene from the Bible
  • B) it was the first French painting shown at a public exhibition
  • C) it evoked Roman republican virtue, implicitly critiquing aristocratic decadence in pre-Revolutionary France
  • D) it celebrated the French monarchy
C is correct. it evoked Roman republican virtue, implicitly critiquing aristocratic decadence in pre-Revolutionary France
Q2
Francisco Goya's "Third of May 1808" (1814) was revolutionary in war imagery because
  • A) it portrayed the victims of war with pathos rather than glorifying military violence: an anti-heroic perspective
  • B) it was the first painting to depict a modern firearm
  • C) it celebrated the French invaders as liberators
  • D) it showed no human figures, only destroyed buildings
A is correct. it portrayed the victims of war with pathos rather than glorifying military violence: an anti-heroic perspective
Q3
Gustave Courbet's "A Burial at Ornans" (1849) was controversial because
  • A) it was painted on an extremely small canvas
  • B) it depicted ordinary provincial people at a burial with the scale and seriousness reserved for epic history painting
  • C) it showed naked figures in a public setting
  • D) it depicted a violent political assassination
B is correct. it depicted ordinary provincial people at a burial with the scale and seriousness reserved for epic history painting
Q4
Édouard Manet's "Olympia" (1865) scandalized Paris because
  • A) it was the first French painting of a female nude
  • B) it depicted a historical goddess in an irreverent context
  • C) it used photographic techniques rather than traditional painting
  • D) it depicted a nude woman as a modern prostitute who stares boldly at the viewer, challenging academic idealization of the nude
D is correct. it depicted a nude woman as a modern prostitute who stares boldly at the viewer, challenging academic idealization of the nude
Q5
Paul Cézanne is considered a "bridge" to Modernism because
  • A) he was the first painter to use photographs as references
  • B) his analysis of form through geometric simplification and multiple viewpoints directly influenced Cubism and 20th-century abstraction
  • C) he invented the Impressionist style of loose brushwork
  • D) he founded the Surrealist movement
B is correct. his analysis of form through geometric simplification and multiple viewpoints directly influenced Cubism and 20th-century abstraction
Q6
Wassily Kandinsky's move toward pure abstraction (c. 1910) was motivated by
  • A) the influence of Cubism and Picasso's work directly
  • B) his desire to mimic musical composition visually without a theory
  • C) his belief that colors and forms could express spiritual and emotional states without reference to recognizable objects
  • D) his inability to draw the human figure accurately
C is correct. his belief that colors and forms could express spiritual and emotional states without reference to recognizable objects
Q7
The Dada movement (1916 to 1920s) responded to World War I by
  • A) reviving Renaissance ideals as a cultural cure
  • B) creating technically virtuosic works in the academic tradition
  • C) creating deliberately irrational, provocative, and anti-art works to critique the culture that produced the war
  • D) celebrating nationalist military values through heroic imagery
C is correct. creating deliberately irrational, provocative, and anti-art works to critique the culture that produced the war
Q8
Jackson Pollock's "drip paintings" (late 1940s) are best analyzed as
  • A) landscapes of the American West seen from above
  • B) portraits with the human figure dissolved into color
  • C) process-based works where the act of painting (pouring, dripping, moving around the canvas) is itself the subject
  • D) carefully planned geometric compositions executed spontaneously
C is correct. process-based works where the act of painting (pouring, dripping, moving around the canvas) is itself the subject
Q9
Pop Art (1950s to 60s, Warhol, Lichtenstein) used imagery from
  • A) prehistoric cave paintings and tribal art
  • B) scientific diagrams and architectural blueprints
  • C) Renaissance religious subjects reinterpreted for modern audiences
  • D) mass media, advertising, and consumer culture to comment on (and celebrate or critique) postwar American consumerism
D is correct. mass media, advertising, and consumer culture to comment on (and celebrate or critique) postwar American consumerism
Q10
The Feminist Art movement (1970s) challenged art history by
  • A) arguing that women artists had been systematically excluded and that art history's canon reflected patriarchal values
  • B) claiming all great art was created by women
  • C) rejecting all figurative art as inherently gendered
  • D) arguing that gender was irrelevant to artistic judgment
A is correct. arguing that women artists had been systematically excluded and that art history's canon reflected patriarchal values

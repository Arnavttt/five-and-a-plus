Contemporary Art: Identity, Globalization, and New Media

Learn on this site
Identity and Postcolonial Art

Contemporary art since the 1980s has centered on questions of identity: race (Jean-Michel Basquiat, Kara Walker, Kerry James Marshall), gender and sexuality (Cindy Sherman, Zanele Muholi), diaspora and postcolonialism (Yinka Shonibare, Cai Guo-Qiang, El Anatsui). These artists use appropriation, photography, installation, and performance to challenge Western art historical canons and address ongoing legacies of colonialism.

Common misconception: Students assume contemporary art is purely conceptual with no craft; many contemporary artists possess exceptional technical skill alongside conceptual depth.

How it can be tested: Analyze the use of identity in contemporary art; explain postcolonial critique; describe how artists use non-traditional media to challenge art historical assumptions.

Installation Art, Biennials, and Digital Media

Installation art creates immersive environments the viewer inhabits: examples include Judy Chicago's "The Dinner Party" (1974 to 1979), Kara Walker's "A Subtlety" (2014), and Ai Weiwei's "Sunflower Seeds" (2010). International biennials (Venice, São Paulo, Gwangju) have created a global infrastructure for contemporary art. Digital art (net art, AI art, NFTs) raises new questions about originality and authorship.

Common misconception: Students assume the art world is centered exclusively in New York and London; since the 1990s, biennials across Asia, Africa, and Latin America have shifted the art world's geography.

How it can be tested: Describe the biennial as an art world institution; explain what installation art demands of its viewer; analyze how digital art challenges definitions of the art object.

Key Vocabulary
  • installation art: An art form creating an immersive three-dimensional environment for the viewer to inhabit; often site-specific and temporary
  • appropriation: The use of pre-existing imagery, objects, or styles in new artworks, often to critique or recontextualize the original
  • postcolonialism: A critical framework examining the cultural and artistic legacies of European colonialism
  • biennial: A large-scale international contemporary art exhibition held every two years; Venice Biennale (est. 1895) is the oldest
  • diaspora art: Art by artists living outside their ancestral homeland, often negotiating between multiple cultural identities
  • NFT: Non-fungible token: a blockchain-based certificate of ownership for a digital artwork; raises questions of originality and value
Objectives
  • Explain how contemporary artists use identity as subject matter
  • Analyze the significance of international biennials for global art
  • Describe what installation art demands of the viewer
How this can be tested
  • How does Kara Walker use the silhouette form to address American racial history?
  • What is Ai Weiwei's significance as both artist and political dissident?
Practice before moving on
  • Is digital art "real" art? What criteria should we use to evaluate it?
  • How does the art market influence what we consider great contemporary art?

Q1
Jean-Michel Basquiat's paintings (1980s New York) are significant for
  • A) introducing abstract expressionism to American art
  • B) being the first artist to exhibit street art in a gallery
  • C) creating purely decorative paintings with no political content
  • D) combining graffiti, African-American experience, art history, and social critique in a neo-expressionist style that challenged the predominantly white art market
D is correct. combining graffiti, African-American experience, art history, and social critique in a neo-expressionist style that challenged the predominantly white art market
Q2
Kara Walker's large-scale silhouettes address
  • A) the Victorian silhouette tradition without political commentary
  • B) the history of American slavery and racial violence: using the decorative silhouette form to depict traumatic scenes, creating jarring contrast between elegant form and brutal content
  • C) the experience of African immigration to the United States in the 20th century
  • D) the history of abstract painting in America
B is correct. the history of American slavery and racial violence: using the decorative silhouette form to depict traumatic scenes, creating jarring contrast between elegant form and brutal content
Q3
International biennials have shifted contemporary art by
  • A) creating a global platform presenting non-Western artists alongside Western artists: decentralizing New York and London as sole arbiters of important art
  • B) concentrating all important contemporary art in Western European cities
  • C) replacing national museums as the primary exhibition venue
  • D) focusing exclusively on emerging artists under 30
A is correct. creating a global platform presenting non-Western artists alongside Western artists: decentralizing New York and London as sole arbiters of important art
Q4
Installation art differs from traditional painting and sculpture in that it
  • A) uses no physical materials, existing only as concept
  • B) is always temporary and never documented
  • C) must fill an entire building to qualify as installation
  • D) implicates the viewer's body in the work: the viewer inhabits the space rather than observing from outside
D is correct. implicates the viewer's body in the work: the viewer inhabits the space rather than observing from outside
Q5
El Anatsui's (Ghana) metal tapestries made from liquor bottle caps are significant because
  • A) they represent purely formal exploration of color with no historical content
  • B) they were made using unmodified traditional Ghanaian metalworking techniques
  • C) the bottle caps are used only for color and texture with no historical meaning
  • D) they reference the history of the alcohol trade in colonial Africa while using a postcolonial material in a form referencing both African textiles and Western abstract painting
D is correct. they reference the history of the alcohol trade in colonial Africa while using a postcolonial material in a form referencing both African textiles and Western abstract painting

⚡ Common Mistakes & Exam Tips
  • Attributing the Cold War's end solely to Reagan's military buildup historians debate the relative importance of Reagan's pressure vs. Soviet internal dysfunction vs. Gorbachev's choices.
  • Describing Reaganomics as simply 'tax cuts' the exam rewards understanding supply-side theory: cuts to top marginal rates were supposed to 'trickle down' through investment, not directly through redistribution.
  • Exam tip: For LEQs on the conservative movement, trace continuity from Goldwater (1964) through Nixon's Southern Strategy to Reagan's coalition show the long-term political realignment.

The "global turn" in contemporary art since the 1990s means
  • A) the art market has declined in New York and London
  • B) art history now must account for artists from Asia, Africa, Latin America, and the Pacific as equal contributors: not as "world art" footnotes to a Western-centered narrative

Q2
Cindy Sherman's "Untitled Film Stills" (1977 to 1980) are significant for
  • A) celebrating classic Hollywood femininity as a positive cultural ideal
  • B) using photography and self-portraiture to critique how cinema and media construct images of femininity: Sherman plays multiple stereotyped female roles, exposing their artificiality
  • C) being honest documentary photographs of women in everyday situations
  • D) critiquing the film industry's casting practices through interviews
B is correct. using photography and self-portraiture to critique how cinema and media construct images of femininity: Sherman plays multiple stereotyped female roles, exposing their artificiality
Q3
Ai Weiwei's art and activism are inseparable because
  • A) his work directly challenges Chinese government authority: "Sunflower Seeds," "Remembering," and his documentation of earthquake deaths constitute both art and political action
  • B) he creates purely formalist art with no political content
  • C) his activism occurs entirely outside his artistic practice
  • D) he works exclusively within Chinese government-approved institutions
A is correct. his work directly challenges Chinese government authority: "Sunflower Seeds," "Remembering," and his documentation of earthquake deaths constitute both art and political action
Q4
The concept of "cultural appropriation" is debated in contemporary art because
  • A) it applies only to commercial mass media, not fine art
  • B) it raises questions about who has the right to use symbols and traditions from cultures not their own: distinguishing respectful exchange from exploitative borrowing
  • C) all cultural exchange in art is automatically appropriation regardless of context
  • D) appropriation is universally celebrated as a sign of cultural exchange
B is correct. it raises questions about who has the right to use symbols and traditions from cultures not their own: distinguishing respectful exchange from exploitative borrowing
Q5
Zanele Muholi's photographic portraits (South Africa) are significant for
  • A) using classical European portrait traditions without modification
  • B) documenting South African wildlife and landscape
  • C) working exclusively in abstract photography
  • D) documenting and celebrating Black LGBTQ+ identity in South Africa: creating a counter-archive against invisibility and violence, grounding art in human rights
D is correct. documenting and celebrating Black LGBTQ+ identity in South Africa: creating a counter-archive against invisibility and violence, grounding art in human rights
Q6
The rise of street art (Banksy, Shepard Fairey) as a recognized art form has
  • A) resulted in street artists permanently abandoning public spaces for galleries
  • B) proven that only formally trained artists can produce meaningful public art
  • C) challenged gallery-system gatekeeping: demonstrating that artistic value and political critique can exist outside institutional validation in public space
  • D) been universally condemned by art institutions as mere graffiti
C is correct. challenged gallery-system gatekeeping: demonstrating that artistic value and political critique can exist outside institutional validation in public space
Q7
Postcolonial art criticism asks us to consider
  • A) whether postcolonial countries should develop entirely separate art histories
  • B) how Western museums, art history, and the market have systematically excluded, misrepresented, or appropriated non-Western art: and what reparative histories we need to tell
  • C) whether art from colonized regions is technically sophisticated enough for Western museums
  • D) how contemporary art movements derived from African and Asian traditions
B is correct. how Western museums, art history, and the market have systematically excluded, misrepresented, or appropriated non-Western art: and what reparative histories we need to tell
Q8
Yinka Shonibare's use of Dutch wax-print fabric is a postcolonial critique because
  • A) the choice of fabric is purely aesthetic with no political implications
  • B) the fabric: marketed as "African": was industrially produced in the Netherlands for African markets; using it exposes the fabricated nature of cultural identity under colonialism
  • C) the fabric is an authentic pre-colonial Yoruba handwoven textile
  • D) Shonibare uses it as a tribute to Dutch-African trade relations
B is correct. the fabric: marketed as "African": was industrially produced in the Netherlands for African markets; using it exposes the fabricated nature of cultural identity under colonialism
Q9
The debate about NFTs and digital art in art history centers on
  • A) questions of originality, authenticity, authorship, and the role of scarcity in creating value: the same questions Duchamp's readymades raised in 1917
  • B) whether digital art can ever have aesthetic value
  • C) the environmental impact of blockchain technology exclusively
  • D) whether digital artworks can be physically touched
A is correct. questions of originality, authenticity, authorship, and the role of scarcity in creating value: the same questions Duchamp's readymades raised in 1917
Q10
A postmodern approach to art history is characterized by
  • A) skepticism toward master narratives, recognition of multiple equally valid traditions, attention to power relationships in what gets displayed, and questioning of aesthetic absolutes
  • B) celebrating the superiority of Western artistic achievement over all other traditions
  • C) returning to Renaissance ideals of beauty and proportion
  • D) rejecting all interpretation of art in favor of purely formal description
A is correct. skepticism toward master narratives, recognition of multiple equally valid traditions, attention to power relationships in what gets displayed, and questioning of aesthetic absolutes
